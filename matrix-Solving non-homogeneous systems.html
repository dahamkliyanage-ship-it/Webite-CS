Solving non-homogeneous systems

Consider the non-homogeneous systems
$$ A_{n \times n}X_{n \times 1} = B_{n \times 1}. $$
  • If $|A| \neq 0$ then the system has a unique solution. (i.e. $\text{Rank } A = \text{Rank}(A|B) = n$)
  • If $|A| = 0$ then the system has no solutions or infinitely many solutions.
    • If $\text{Rank } A < \text{Rank}(A|B)$ then the system has no solutions.
    • If $\text{Rank } A = \text{Rank}(A|B) < n$ then the system has infinitely many solutions.
Definition:

If the system $AX = B$ has at least one solution then we say it is consistent. Otherwise, it is inconsistent.
Theorem: Consider the non-homogeneous systems
$$ A_{m \times n}X_{n \times 1} = B_{m \times 1}. $$
  • If $\text{Rank } A = \text{Rank}(A|B) = n$ then the system has a unique solution.
  • If $\text{Rank } A = \text{Rank}(A|B) < n$ then the system has infinitely many solutions.
  • If $\text{Rank } A \neq \text{Rank}(A|B)$ then the system has no solutions.
Example 3. Show that the following system has a unique solution unless $a = 2$.
$x - y + 2z = -3$
$2x + 3y - 4z = 5$
$5x + az = b$.
Solution:
Observe that
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & \mid & -3 \\ 2 & 3 & -4 & \mid & 5 \\ 5 & 0 & a & \mid & b \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & \mid & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & -8/5 & \mid & 11/5 \\ 0 & 0 & a-2 & \mid & b+4 \end{pmatrix}. $$
If $a \neq 2$ then
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & \mid & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & -8/5 & \mid & 11/5 \\ 0 & 0 & a-2 & \mid & b+4 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & \mid & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & -8/5 & \mid & 11/5 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \mid & \frac{b+4}{a-2} \end{pmatrix} $$
and $\text{Rank } A = \text{Rank}(A|B) = 3$.

Therefore the system has a unique solution if $a \neq 2$.
