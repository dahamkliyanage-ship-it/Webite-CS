Pin-Jointed Trusses

ME 1033 - Statics & Trusses Summary

1. Structural Elements & Internal Forces

  • Bars (1-D): Carry axial force (N), shear forces (Sy, Sz), torque (T), and bending moments (My, Mz).
  • Axially Loaded Bars (Trusses):
    • Ties: Predominant tension.
    • Struts: Predominant compression.
  • Flexural vs Torsional: Beams carry bending; Shafts carry torque.
  • Plates & Shells (2-D): Slabs (plane) and tanks/domes (curved).
  • Blocks (3-D): Massive structures (pyramids, stupas).
  • Ideal Truss Assumptions: Members connected by frictionless pin joints at ends; loads applied only at joints; members develop purely axial forces.

2. Stability & Determinacy Conditions

The necessary (but not always sufficient) conditions for a simply stiff structure:

Closed 2-D Truss: m = 2j - 3
Open 2-D Truss: m = 2j
Closed 3-D Space Truss: m = 3j - 6
Open 3-D Space Truss: m = 3j
  • m < mreqd: Unstable (Mechanism).
  • m = mreqd: Statically Determinate (if geometry is properly triangulated).
  • m > mreqd: Statically Indeterminate (Redundant members exist).
  • Arrangement Precaution: Even if m = 2j - 3, bad member geometry can still make a truss unstable.

3. Special Joints & Zero-Force Members

Quick inspection rules to spot forces without calculating reactions:

  • Collinear Pair + Non-collinear Third Member:
    • Two collinear members carry equal forces (TC = TD).
    • If no external load acts at the joint, the third member is a Zero-Force Member (TB = 0).
    • If an external load P acts along the third member, its internal force equals P (TB = -P).
  • Two Non-collinear Members (No External Load): Both members carry zero force (TC = 0, TD = 0).

4. Truss Analysis: Joints vs Sections

  • Method of Joints:
    • Based on equilibrium of individual joints (ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0).
    • Start at a joint with at most 2 unknown member forces.
    • Assume tension (pulling away from joint); negative result indicates compression.
  • Method of Sections:
    • Based on overall equilibrium of an isolated part of the truss.
    • Pass an imaginary cut through not more than 3 members whose internal forces are needed.
    • Solve using 3 planar equilibrium equations: ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0, ΣM = 0.

5. Beam Analogy Method (Approximate)

Used for fast estimates on parallel-chorded trusses / open web girders of depth d:

Mmax = wL² / 8 = Fchord · d → Fchord(max) = Mmax / d
Vmax = wL / 2 = Fweb · sin θ → Fweb(max) = Vmax / sin θ
  • Chord Members: Carry overall bending moments (tension in bottom chord, compression in top chord for sagging).
  • Web Members: Vertical component resists total shear force.

6. Statically Indeterminate Trusses

  • Limitation: Equilibrium equations alone cannot solve member forces (more unknowns than available equations).
  • Compatibility Requirement: Must use deformation compatibility equations combined with Hooke's Law:
Elongation: e = (F · L) / (A · E)
Compatibility: Geometric relation between joint displacement Δ and member elongations ei
  • Stiffness Influence: Member forces depend directly on cross-sectional areas (A) and Young's Modulus (E).

Quick Test: Truss Stability & Zero-Force

A planar closed truss has 6 joints and 8 members. Is this truss stable? At an unloaded joint with 3 members, two are collinear. What is the force in the non-collinear member?

Show Answer & Working

1. Required members for stability: m = 2j - 3 = 2(6) - 3 = 9. Since provided m = 8 < 9, the truss is Unstable (Mechanism).

2. By special joint inspection, the non-collinear third member carries Zero Force.
