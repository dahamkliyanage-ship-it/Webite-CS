The Real Number System & Axioms

Why Does $\mathbb{R}$ Need Three Pillars?

Think of building a high-precision measurement system:

  • The Field Axioms give you your arithmetic engine: you can reliably add, subtract, multiply, and scale values without formulas collapsing.
  • The Order Axioms give you direction and comparison: you can tell which reading is larger, calibrate sensor thresholds, and define error margins using absolute values.
  • The Completeness Axiom is the solid concrete: it glues all points together into an unbroken continuum so limits never fall into empty space.

Part A: The Field Axioms (Algebraic Foundations)

Axiom 1 (Field Axioms): $\mathbb{R}$ is a non-empty set with addition (+) and multiplication ($\cdot$) satisfying 9 rules:

  • Commutativity of Addition: $\forall a,b \in \mathbb{R}, a+b=b+a$.
  • Associativity of Addition: $\forall a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}, a+(b+c)=(a+b)+c$.
  • Additive Identity: $\exists 0 \in \mathbb{R}, \forall a \in \mathbb{R}, a+0=a$.
  • Additive Inverse: $\forall a \in \mathbb{R}, \exists -a \in \mathbb{R}, a+(-a)=0$.
  • Commutativity of Multiplication: $\forall a,b \in \mathbb{R}, a \cdot b=b \cdot a$.
  • Associativity of Multiplication: $\forall a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}, a \cdot (b \cdot c)=(a \cdot b) \cdot c$.
  • Multiplicative Identity: $\exists 1 \in \mathbb{R}, \forall a \in \mathbb{R}, a \cdot 1=a$.
  • Multiplicative Inverse: $\forall a \in \mathbb{R}-\{0\}, \exists a^{-1} \in \mathbb{R}, a \cdot a^{-1}=1$.
  • Distributive Law: $\forall a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}, a \cdot (b+c)=(a \cdot b)+(a \cdot c)$.

Number Hierarchy & Fields:

  • $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ are fields because they satisfy all 9 axioms.
  • $\mathbb{Z}$ with + and $\cdot$ is not a field because it fails Axiom 8 (multiplicative inverses do not exist in $\mathbb{Z}$; e.g., $2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2} \notin \mathbb{Z}$).

Part B: The Order Axioms (Linear Continuum)

Axiom 2 (Order Axioms): $\mathbb{R}$ has a strict linear order relation $<$ satisfying:

  • Trichotomy: For any $a,b \in \mathbb{R}$, exactly one holds: $a=b$, $a
  • Transitivity: $\forall a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$, $a
  • Addition Invariance: $\forall a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$, $a
  • Multiplication by Positives: $\forall a,b,c \in \mathbb{R}$, $a

Important Absolute Value Properties:

  • $\vert{}a\vert{} \le r \Leftrightarrow -r \le a \le r$.
  • Triangle Inequality: $\vert{}a\vert{}-\vert{}b\vert{} \le \vert{}a+b\vert{} \le \vert{}a\vert{}+\vert{}b\vert{}$.
  • Parallelogram Identity: $\vert{}a+b\vert{}^2 + \vert{}a-b\vert{}^2 = 2a^2 + 2b^2$.

Part C: The Completeness Axiom & Bounds

  • Upper Bound: $u \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $\forall a \in A, a \le u$.
  • Lower Bound: $l \in \mathbb{R}$ such that $\forall a \in A, l \le a$.
  • Supremum ($sup~A$): The least upper bound of A. If $u < sup~A$, then $u$ is not an upper bound of A.
  • Infimum ($inf~A$): The greatest lower bound of A. If $l > inf~A$, then $l$ is not a lower bound of A.

Axiom 3 (The Completeness Axiom):

  • Every non-empty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ that is bounded above has a supremum in $\mathbb{R}$.
  • Every non-empty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ that is bounded below has an infimum in $\mathbb{R}$.

Key Theorems & Proofs

  • Archimedean Property: For every $a \in \mathbb{R}$, there exists $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $n \ge a$. (Proved by showing $\mathbb{Z}$ is unbounded above using the Completeness Axiom).
  • Density of $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}-\mathbb{Q}$: There is a rational and an irrational between any two reals.
  • Supremum Addition: For $A+B = \{a+b \vert{} a \in A, b \in B\}$, $sup(A+B) = sup~A + sup~B$.
  • $\epsilon$-Characterization of Supremum: Let A be bounded above by $u$. Then $\forall \epsilon>0, \exists a \in A$ such that $a+\epsilon > u \Leftrightarrow u = sup~A$.
