ME 1033: 2D Kinetics (Particles & Rigid Bodies)

Equations of Motion, D'Alembert's Dynamic Equilibrium, System Interconnections & Centre of Percussion

1. Particle Kinetics (Newton's Second Law)

Relates unbalanced resultant forces to linear acceleration measured from an inertial frame of reference.

Vector Form: ΣF = m · a

Cartesian Coordinates:
ΣFx = m · ax = m · ẍ
ΣFy = m · ay = m · ÿ

Plane-Polar Coordinates:
ΣFr = m · ar = m · (r̈ - r · θ̇²)  [Centripetal force inward]
ΣFθ = m · aθ = m · (r · θ̈ + 2 · ṙ · θ̇)  [Includes Coriolis effect]
Quick Test: IC Engine Piston Force (Lecture 5 Example 1)

A piston (diameter 100 mm, mass 1.6 kg) accelerates upwards at 2860 m/s² with cylinder gas pressure 1.1 N/mm², normal reaction N = 2000 N, and friction μ = 0.17. What is the total vertical force required from the connecting rod?

Show Answer & Working

1. Gas force (downward): P = p · A = 1.1 × π/4 × (100)² = 8639.4 N.

2. Friction (downward): Ff = μ · N = 0.17 × 2000 = 340 N.

3. Weight (downward): W = mg = 1.6 × 9.81 ≈ 15.7 N.

4. Dynamic equilibrium (↑+): Frod,y - P - Ff - W = m · a.

Frod,y = 8639.4 + 340 + 15.7 + (1.6 × 2860) = 8995.1 + 4576 = 13571 N.

2. D'Alembert's Principle & Dynamic Equilibrium

Converts an acceleration problem into an equivalent static equilibrium system by introducing an imaginary reversed effective force (inertia force).

ΣF - m · a = 0  →  Dynamic Equilibrium Form
Inertia Force Vector: Finertia = -m · a
Inertia Moment (Couple): Minertia = -IG · α
  • Static Analogy: By treating -m·a as an applied external force, standard statics equations (ΣF = 0, ΣM = 0) can be directly applied.
  • System of Particles: ΣFext = mtotal · aG, where G is the center of mass of the combined cluster.

3. Kinetics of a Rigid Body

Because rigid bodies possess physical dimensions, non-concurrent forces trigger simultaneous translation and rotation.

Translational Equation: ΣF = m · aG
Rotational Equation about Mass Centre G: ΣMG = IG · α
Rotational Equation about Arbitrary Point O: ΣMO = IG · α + (m · aG) · rOG
Mass Moment of Inertia: IG = m · kG²  [kG = radius of gyration]
Total Kinetic Energy: T = (1/2) · m · vG² + (1/2) · IG · ω²
Quick Test: Unbalanced Wheel (Lecture 5 Example 3)

A 30 kg wheel (radius R = 0.25 m, kG = 0.15 m) has its center of mass G located 0.1 m horizontally from its geometric center O. If released from rest with no slipping, what is its initial angular acceleration α?

Show Answer & Working

1. Contact point I is the instantaneous centre (no slip, ω = 0).

2. Mass moment of inertia about I: II = m · kG² + m · (GI)² = 30(0.15² + [0.1² + 0.25²]) = 30(0.0225 + 0.0725) = 2.85 kg·m².

3. Torque about I: ΣMI = mg · (0.1) = 30 × 9.81 × 0.1 = 29.43 N·m.

4. ΣMI = II · α → 29.43 = 2.85 · α → α = 10.3 rad/s².

4. Systems of Interconnected Rigid Bodies

For assemblies connected by hinges or pins, internal reactions cancel out when analyzing the complete system Free-Body Diagram alongside its Kinetic Diagram.

Resultant Force Equivalence: ΣF = Σ(mi · aGi)
Moment Equivalence about Pivot P: ΣMP = Σ(Ii · αi) + Σ(mi · aGi · di)
  • Eliminates tedious joint reaction calculations at connecting hinge pins.
  • Combines kinematic constraints (from velocity/acceleration polygons) directly into dynamic force equilibrium.

5. Centre of Percussion (Sweet Spot)

The Centre of Percussion (COP) is a point on a pinned/pivoted rigid body where a transverse impact produces zero impulsive reaction force at the supporting pivot.

Distance from Pivot O to COP (q): q = OG + LG
Distance below Mass Centre (LG): LG = kG² / OG

For a Uniform Thin Rod of Length L pivoted at one end:
OG = L / 2
kG² = L² / 12
LG = (L² / 12) / (L / 2) = L / 6
Total Distance from Pivot: q = L/2 + L/6 = (2/3) · L
  • Dynamic Cancellation: Linear inertia force (m · aG) and rotational inertia reaction (IG · α) completely balance each other at the pivot point.
  • Engineering & Sports Applications: Striking faces of hammers, mallets, swords, cricket bats, baseball bats, and tennis racquets minimize vibration and wrist shock.

Quick Test: Centre of Percussion

A uniform wooden meter ruler (length L = 1.0 m) is suspended from a pin at its extreme upper end. At what distance from the pin should it be struck so that no reaction force is transmitted to the support pin?

Show Answer & Working

For a uniform bar pivoted at an end: q = (2/3) · L = (2/3) × 1.0 m = 0.667 m (66.7 cm) from the pivot.
