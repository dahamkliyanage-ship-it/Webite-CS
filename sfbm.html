Internal Forces in Beams

ME 1033 - Statics & Structural Mechanics Summary

1. Beam Basics & Support Configurations

  • Beam Characteristics: Long, straight, prismatic members subjected primarily to transverse loads causing bending and shear.
  • Prismatic Beam: Cross-sectional dimensions remain uniform throughout the length (I = (1/12) · b · h³ = constant).
  • Degrees of Freedom (2-D Plane Member): Total of 3 DOFs — Translation along x, Translation along y, Rotation about z.
  • Common Beam Configurations:
    • Simply Supported: Pinned at one end, roller at the other (Statically Determinate).
    • Cantilever: Fixed at one end, completely free at the other (Statically Determinate).
    • Overhanging: Extends beyond supports (Statically Determinate).
    • Propped Cantilever / Continuous / Fixed: Additional redundant supports (Statically Indeterminate).

2. Support Reactions & Restraints

  • Roller Support: Restrains 1 DOF (perpendicular translation); provides 1 vertical reaction (Ry).
  • Pinned Support: Restrains 2 DOFs (translations in x and y); provides 2 reaction components (Rx, Ry).
  • Fixed Support: Restrains all 3 DOFs (x, y translations and z rotation); provides Rx, Ry, and a reaction moment (M).

3. Standard Sign Conventions

  • Bending Moment (BM):
    • Hogging (+): Tension at top fibres, compression at bottom fibres.
    • Sagging (-): Tension at bottom fibres, compression at top fibres.
  • Shear Force (SF):
    • Clockwise (+): Tendency to rotate the beam segment clockwise (Left-Up / Right-Down).
    • Counter-Clockwise (-): Tendency to rotate the beam segment counter-clockwise (Left-Down / Right-Up).

4. Differential Relations (Load, SF & BM)

Mathematical link across load intensity w(x), shear force S(x), and bending moment M(x):

dS / dx = -w(x)  →  [Slope of SF diagram = -Load intensity]
dM / dx = -S(x)  →  [Slope of BM diagram = -Shear force]
d²M / dx² = w(x)
  • Key Curve Behaviors:
    • Where load w = 0 → SF is horizontal/constant; BM is a straight inclined line.
    • Where UDL w = constant → SF varies linearly; BM varies as a quadratic parabola.
    • Where load is triangular (linear) → SF is parabolic; BM varies cubically.
    • Critical Stationary BM: Maximum/minimum bending moment occurs where dM/dx = 0, meaning precisely where S(x) = 0.

5. Principle of Superposition

For linearly elastic structures with small deflections, combined loading effects can be evaluated by summing independent sub-cases:

Total SF: S(x) = SUDL(x) + SPointLoad(x)
Total BM: M(x) = MUDL(x) + MPointLoad(x)
  • Complex loading is partitioned into standard load patterns.
  • Resulting internal force diagrams are formed by algebraic point-by-point summation.

Quick Test: SFD & BMD Relationships

A simply supported beam carries a uniformly distributed load w. If the shear force diagram crosses zero (S = 0) at midspan, what happens to the bending moment at that exact point?

Show Answer & Working

1. Since dM/dx = -S, when S = 0 the slope of the BM diagram is zero (dM/dx = 0).

2. Therefore, the Maximum Bending Moment occurs at this exact location.
