Topic 03

Crystal Systems & Lattices

Every crystalline material in existence falls into one of 7 possible crystal systems, which are defined by the geometry of their unit cell[cite: 12, 13].

  • Cubic: a = b = c, and α = β = γ = 90°
  • Hexagonal: a = b ≠ c, and α = β = 90°, γ = 120°
  • Tetragonal: a = b ≠ c, and α = β = γ = 90°
  • Rhombohedral (Trigonal): a = b = c, and α = β = γ ≠ 90°
  • Orthorhombic: a ≠ b ≠ c, and α = β = γ = 90°
  • Monoclinic: a ≠ b ≠ c, and α = γ = 90° ≠ β
  • Triclinic: a ≠ b ≠ c, and α ≠ β ≠ γ ≠ 90°

While there are only 7 base systems, unit cells can have additional lattice points beyond just the corners (such as Body-Centered, Face-Centered, or Base-Centered)[cite: 12]. Mathematician Auguste Bravais proved that combining the 7 crystal systems with these variations results in exactly 14 possible Bravais Lattices[cite: 12].
