Properties of Vectors

Theorem 1.4.1: Suppose \(\underline{a} = a_1\underline{i} + a_2\underline{j}\) and \(\underline{b} = b_1\underline{i} + b_2\underline{j}\). Then

(i). \(\underline{a} + \underline{b} = (a_1 + b_1)\underline{i} + (a_2 + b_2)\underline{j}\).

(ii). \(k\underline{a} = ka_1\underline{i} + ka_2\underline{j}\) where \(k\) is a scalar.

(iii). \(|\underline{a}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2}\).

(iv). \(\underline{a} - \underline{b} = (a_1 - b_1)\underline{i} + (a_2 - b_2)\underline{j}\).

Vectors may also be represented by components. That is \(\underline{a} = a_1\underline{i} + a_2\underline{j} = \langle a_1, a_2 \rangle\).

Then we have:

  • \(\underline{a} + \underline{b} = \langle a_1 + b_1, a_2 + b_2 \rangle\),
  • \(\underline{a} - \underline{b} = \langle a_1 - b_1, a_2 - b_2 \rangle\), and
  • \(k\underline{a} = \langle ka_1, ka_2 \rangle\).

These concepts can also be extended to higher dimensions.

Application: Structural Mechanics
Determining the net force on a mechanical joint requires adding vector components. Suppose a structural node experiences two force vectors:

  • Load A: \(\underline{F_1} = 3\underline{i} + 4\underline{j}\) kN
  • Load B: \(\underline{F_2} = 5\underline{i} - 2\underline{j}\) kN
Using Theorem 1.4.1, the resultant combined force \(\underline{R}\) acting on the node is:
\(\underline{R} = \underline{F_1} + \underline{F_2} = (3 + 5)\underline{i} + (4 - 2)\underline{j} = 8\underline{i} + 2\underline{j}\) kN
To find the absolute magnitude of this structural load, we apply Theorem 1.4.1 (iii):
\(|\underline{R}| = \sqrt{8^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{64 + 4} = \sqrt{68} \approx 8.25\) kN

Theorem 1.4.2 (Ratio Formula): Let \(A\) and \(B\) be two points with position vectors \(\underline{a}\) and \(\underline{b}\) respectively. If \(C\) is a point that divides \(AB\) in the ratio of \(m : n\) then the position vector of \(C\) is given by
\(\underline{c} = \frac{n\underline{a} + m\underline{b}}{m + n}\).

Applying the Ratio Formula: A common engineering application of this formula is finding the exact midpoint of a structural element, such as locating the center of an elastic beam resting between supports \(A\) and \(B\). For a midpoint, the ratio \(m:n\) is exactly \(1:1\). Substituting \(m=1\) and \(n=1\) into the formula simplifies directly to:

\(\underline{c} = \frac{1\underline{a} + 1\underline{b}}{1 + 1} = \frac{\underline{a} + \underline{b}}{2}\)
