Definition:

Definition(Beta function):

The definite integral $\int_0^1 x^{m-1}(1 - x)^{n-1}dx$, for $m > 0, n > 0$ is known as the Beta function and is denoted by $B(m, n)$ [read as "Beta m, n"]. The Beta function is also called the Eulerian integral of the first kind.

Thus,
$$ B(m, n) = \int_0^1 x^{m-1}(1 - x)^{n-1}dx, \quad m > 0, n > 0. $$

Symmetrical Property

Symmetrical property of Beta function, i.e., $B(m, n) = B(n, m)$

Proof: By the definition of the Beta function, we have
$$ B(m, n) = \int_0^1 x^{m-1}(1 - x)^{n-1}dx $$
Using the definite integral property $\int_0^a f(x)dx = \int_0^a f(a - x)dx$, substitute $x$ with $(1 - x)$:
$$ = \int_0^1 (1 - x)^{m-1}(1 - (1 - x))^{n-1}dx $$ $$ = \int_0^1 (1 - x)^{m-1}x^{n-1}dx $$ $$ = \int_0^1 x^{n-1}(1 - x)^{m-1}dx = B(n, m) $$
Now by definition of the Beta function, $B(n, m) = B(m, n)$.

Transformation of Beta Function (Forms I - III)

  • Form I: Show that
    $$ B(m, n) = \int_0^\infty \frac{x^{n-1}dx}{(1 + x)^{n+m}} = \int_0^\infty \frac{x^{m-1}dx}{(1 + x)^{n+m}}, \quad m, n > 0 $$
    Proof: Let $x = \frac{1}{(1 + t)}$, so that $dx = -\frac{dt}{(1 + t)^2}$. As $x \to 0, t \to \infty$ and as $x \to 1, t \to 0$.
    Substitute these into the Beta function definition:
    $$ B(m, n) = -\int_\infty^0 \left(\frac{1}{1 + t}\right)^{m-1} \left(1 - \frac{1}{1 + t}\right)^{n-1} \frac{dt}{(1 + t)^2} $$ $$ = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{(1 + t)^{m-1}} \left(\frac{t}{1 + t}\right)^{n-1} \frac{dt}{(1 + t)^2} = \int_0^\infty \frac{t^{n-1}}{(1 + t)^{n+m}} dt $$
    Replacing $t$ with $x$ gives the first result. Since $m, n$ are interchangeable ($B(m,n) = B(n,m)$), it implies the second form as well.
  • Form II: Show that
    $$ B(m, n) = \int_0^1 \frac{x^{m-1} + x^{n-1}}{(1 + x)^{n+m}}dx $$
    Proof 0.7.11: From Form I, split the integral at $1$:
    $$ B(m, n) = \int_0^\infty \frac{x^{m-1}}{(1 + x)^{n+m}}dx = \int_0^1 \frac{x^{m-1}}{(1 + x)^{n+m}}dx + \int_1^\infty \frac{x^{m-1}}{(1 + x)^{n+m}}dx $$
    In the second integral, substitute $x = 1/t$, so $dx = -1/t^2 dt$.
    $$ \int_1^\infty \frac{x^{m-1}}{(1 + x)^{n+m}}dx = \int_1^0 \frac{(1/t)^{m-1}}{(1 + 1/t)^{n+m}} \left(-\frac{1}{t^2}\right)dt = \int_0^1 \frac{t^{n-1}}{(1 + t)^{n+m}}dt $$
    Adding the two integrals back together yields the result.
  • Form III: Show that
    $$ \int_0^\infty \frac{x^{m-1}}{(ax + b)^{n+m}}dx = \frac{B(m, n)}{a^m b^n} $$
    Proof: From Form I, put $x = (at)/b$ so that $dx = (a dt)/b$. Substitute this to retrieve the generalized constants.

Transformations (Forms IV - VII)

  • Form IV: Show that
    $$ \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2m-1}\theta \cos^{2n-1}\theta}{(a \sin^2\theta + b \cos^2\theta)^{m+n}} d\theta = \frac{B(m, n)}{2a^m b^n} $$
    Proof: Put $x = \tan^2\theta$ so that $dx = 2\tan\theta\sec^2\theta d\theta$ into Form III.
  • Form V: Show that
    $$ \int_0^1 \frac{x^{m-1}(1 - x)^{n-1}}{(x + a)^{m+n}}dx = \frac{B(m, n)}{a^n(1 + a)^m} $$
    Proof: Start from Form I and use the substitution $\frac{x}{1 + a} = \frac{t}{t + a}$.
  • Form VI:
    $$ \int_b^a (x - b)^{m-1}(a - x)^{n-1}dx = (a - b)^{m+n-1} B(m, n) $$
  • Form VII: Additional rational bounds
    $$ \int_0^1 \frac{x^{m-1}(1 - x)^{n-1}}{(a + (b - a)x)^{m+n}} = \frac{B(m, n)}{a^n b^m} $$
    $$ \int_0^1 \frac{x^{m-1}(1 - x)^{n-1}}{(b + cx)^{m+n}} = \frac{B(m, n)}{(b + c)^m b^n} $$
Example: Evaluating using Form VI

Evaluate the integral $\int_2^5 (x - 2)^2 (5 - x)^4 dx$.

Solution: We use Form VI: $\int_b^a (x - b)^{m-1}(a - x)^{n-1}dx = (a - b)^{m+n-1} B(m, n)$.
Here, $a = 5$ and $b = 2$.
  • $m - 1 = 2 \implies m = 3$
  • $n - 1 = 4 \implies n = 5$
Substituting these values into the formula gives:
$$ (5 - 2)^{3+5-1} B(3, 5) = 3^7 B(3, 5) $$
Now, calculate $B(3, 5)$:
$$ B(3, 5) = \frac{2! \cdot 4!}{7!} = \frac{2 \cdot 24}{5040} = \frac{48}{5040} = \frac{1}{105} $$
Finally, multiply by $3^7 = 2187$:
$$ \text{Result} = 2187 \cdot \frac{1}{105} = \frac{2187}{105} = \frac{729}{35} $$
