The Angle Between Two Planes

Defining the Planes

Consider two non-parallel planes $A$ and $B$ given by $a_1x + a_2y + a_3z = d$ and $b_1x + b_2y + b_3z = d'$ respectively.

It is easy to see that $\underline{a} = \langle a_1, a_2, a_3 \rangle$ is perpendicular to $A$ and $\underline{b} = \langle b_1, b_2, b_3 \rangle$ is perpendicular to $B$.

Geometric Insight: Just as the angle between two straight lines can be found using their direction vectors, the angle between two intersecting planes is precisely equal to the angle between their respective normal vectors (or its supplement $\pi - \phi$). This reduces a complex 3D surface problem into a straightforward vector dot product.


Figure 7: The angle between two planes is determined by the angle between their normal vectors $\underline{a}$ and $\underline{b}$.

Derivation & Formula

Theorem

Therefore, the angle between $A$ and $B$ is the same as the angle between $\underline{a}$ and $\underline{b}$ or $\pi - (\text{the angle between } \underline{a} \text{ and } \underline{b})$.

Thus, it is $\phi$ or $\pi - \phi$ where
$$ \cos \phi = \frac{\underline{a} \cdot \underline{b}}{|\underline{a}||\underline{b}|} = \frac{a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_3}{\sqrt{(a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2)(b_1^2 + b_2^2 + b_3^2)}} $$

Important Consequence (Orthogonality): Two planes are perpendicular if and only if the dot product of their normal vectors is zero: $a_1 b_1 + a_2 b_2 + a_3 b_3 = 0$. Two planes are parallel if their normal vectors are scalar multiples of each other ($\underline{a} = k\underline{b}$).

Detailed Mathematical Example

Example: Finding the Angle Between Two Planes

Find the acute angle $\phi$ between the two planes given by the Cartesian equations:
Plane 1 ($A$): $2x - y + 2z = 5$
Plane 2 ($B$): $3x + 4y - z = 1$

Step 1: Extract the normal vectors from the coefficients.
  • $\underline{a} = \langle 2, -1, 2 \rangle$
  • $\underline{b} = \langle 3, 4, -1 \rangle$
Step 2: Calculate the dot product ($\underline{a} \cdot \underline{b}$).
$$ \underline{a} \cdot \underline{b} = (2)(3) + (-1)(4) + (2)(-1) = 6 - 4 - 2 = 0 $$
Step 3: Analyze the result.
$$ \cos \phi = \frac{0}{|\underline{a}||\underline{b}|} = 0 \implies \phi = \cos^{-1}(0) = 90^\circ \text{ (or } \frac{\pi}{2} \text{ radians)} $$
Because the dot product of their normal vectors evaluates precisely to $0$, we have proven that Plane 1 and Plane 2 are completely perpendicular to each other!
