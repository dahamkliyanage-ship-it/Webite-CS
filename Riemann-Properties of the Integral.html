Basic Properties of Integrals

Notation: If $a < b$ and $f$ is integrable on $[a, b]$, then $\int_a^b f = -\int_b^a f$.

Theorem(Properties of Integrals):

Assume $f$ and $g$ are integrable on $[a,b]$.
  1. $f + g$ is integrable on $[a,b]$ and $\int_a^b (f + g) = \int_a^b f + \int_a^b g$.
  2. For $k \in \mathbb{R}$, the function $k f$ is integrable and $\int_a^b kf = k\int_a^b f$.
  3. If $m \le f(x) \le M$ on $[a,b]$, then $m(b - a) \le \int_a^b f \le M(b - a)$.
  4. If $f(x) \le g(x)$ on $[a,b]$, then $\int_a^b f \le \int_a^b g$.
  5. The function $|f|$ is integrable and $\left| \int_a^b f \right| \le \int_a^b |f|$.

Proofs of Key Properties

Proof(Property 1: Additivity):
Let $\epsilon > 0$. Then there exists a partition $P_1$ and $P_2$ such that $U(f, P_1) - L(f, P_1) < \frac{\epsilon}{2}$ and $U(g, P_2) - L(g, P_2) < \frac{\epsilon}{2}$. Let $P$ be a common refinement of $P_1$ and $P_2$. Then:
$$ U(f, P) - L(f, P) < \frac{\epsilon}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad U(g, P) - L(g, P) < \frac{\epsilon}{2} $$
Therefore, $U(f+g, P) - L(f+g, P) \le U(f, P) + U(g, P) - L(f, P) - L(g, P) < \epsilon$. Hence, $f+g$ is integrable.
Proof(Property 2: Scalar Multiplication):
  • Case I ($k > 0$): $U(kf, P) - L(kf, P) = k(U(f, P) - L(f, P)) < \epsilon$.
  • Case II ($k = -1$): $U(-f, P) - L(-f, P) = -L(f, P) + U(f, P) < \epsilon$.
  • Case III ($k < 0$): Let $k = -s$ where $s > 0$. Since $f$ is Riemann integrable, $sf$ is integrable by Case I, and $-sf$ is integrable by Case II.
Proof(Properties 3 & 5):
For property 3, for any given partition $P = \{x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n\}$, we have:
$$ m(b - a) = \sum_{k=1}^n m_k(x_k - x_{k-1}) = L(f, P) \le L(f) \le \int_a^b f \le U(f) \le U(f, P) = \sum_{k=1}^n M_k(x_k - x_{k-1}) \le M(b - a) $$
. For property 5, as $-|f| \le f \le |f|$, from the previous property $-\int_a^b |f| \le \int_a^b f \le \int_a^b |f|$. Hence, $\left| \int_a^b f \right| \le \int_a^b |f|$.

Products, Maxima, and Minima

Theorem:
If $f$ and $g$ are Riemann integrable, then $fg$ is also Riemann integrable.

Proof: First, we show that $f^2$ is integrable. Then, utilizing the algebraic identity $fg = \frac{(f+g)^2 - f^2 - g^2}{2}$, we prove that $fg$ is integrable because it is comprised of integrable components.
Theorem:
If $f$ and $g$ are Riemann integrable, then $\text{Max}(f, g)$ and $\text{Min}(f, g)$ are also Riemann integrable.

Proof: By definition, $\text{Max}(f, g) := \frac{|f - g| + f + g}{2}$ and $\text{Min}(f, g) := \frac{-|f - g| + f + g}{2}$. Hence they are integrable.

Interval Subdivisions and Sequential Limits

Theorem(Interval Additivity):
Assume $f : [a,b] \to \mathbb{R}$ is bounded, and let $c \in (a,b)$. Then $f$ is integrable on $[a,b]$ if and only if it is integrable on $[a,c]$ and $[c,b]$. In this case, we have:
$$ \int_a^b f = \int_a^c f + \int_c^b f $$
. Proof: If $f$ is integrable on $[a,b]$, inserting the point $c$ into partition $P$ creates a refinement $P'$ such that $U(f, P') - L(f, P') < \epsilon$. This implies integrability on both sub-intervals.
Theorem:(Sequential Characterization):
A bounded function $f : [a,b] \to \mathbb{R}$ is Riemann integrable if and only if there is a sequence $(P_n)$ of partitions such that:
$$ \lim_{n \to \infty} [U(f; P_n) - L(f; P_n)] = 0 $$
. In that case, $\int_a^b f = \lim_{n \to \infty} U(f; P_n) = \lim_{n \to \infty} L(f; P_n)$.
Theorem(Riemann Sum Convergence):
Let $f$ be integrable over $[a,b]$ and $\epsilon > 0$. Then there exists $\delta > 0$ such that for all partitions $P = \{x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ with $|P| < \delta$:
$$ \left| \int_a^b f - \sum_{j=1}^n f(\zeta_j)I_j \right| < \epsilon $$
. where $\zeta_j \in [x_{j-1}, x_j]$ for $j = 1, 2, \dots, n$.

Example Application

Worked Example: Using Linearity Properties

Suppose we are given that $\int_1^4 f(x) dx = 8$ and $\int_1^4 g(x) dx = 3$. Evaluate the following integral:
$$ \int_1^4 (2f(x) - 3g(x)) dx $$
Step 1: Apply Theorem(Property 1 - Additivity)
We can separate the integral into two parts:
$$ \int_1^4 2f(x) dx + \int_1^4 -3g(x) dx $$
Step 2: Apply Theorem(Property 2 - Scalar Multiplication)
We can pull the constants to the outside of the integrals:
$$ 2\int_1^4 f(x) dx - 3\int_1^4 g(x) dx $$
Step 3: Substitute known values
Substitute the given integral values into the expression:
$$ 2(8) - 3(3) = 16 - 9 = 7 $$
The final evaluated answer is $7$.
