Linear Differential Equations

General Linear ODE of Order $n$

The general linear ODE of order $n$ is defined by:
$$ \frac{d^ny}{dx^n} + p_1(x)\frac{d^{n-1}y}{dx^{n-1}} + \dots + p_n(x)y = q(x) $$
  • If $q(x) = 0$, this equation is called a homogeneous equation or reduced equation.
  • If $q(x) \neq 0$, this equation is called nonhomogeneous.

Complementary & Particular Solutions
The general solution to the associated homogeneous equation is called the complementary function $y_c = c_1y_1 + \dots + c_ny_n$, where $y_i$'s are linearly independent solutions.

Once finding $n$ independent solutions from the associated homogeneous equation, the general solution to the original nonhomogeneous ODE can be expressed as:
$$ y = y_p + y_c $$
where $y_p$ is called a particular solution.

Linear Differential Operators with Constant Coefficients

From now on, we consider cases with constant coefficients:
$$ \frac{d^ny}{dx^n} + a_1\frac{d^{n-1}y}{dx^{n-1}} + \dots + a_ny = q(x) $$
Using the differentiation operator $D^i := \frac{d^i}{dx^i}$, we can write the equation as a polynomial operator acting on $y$:
$$ (D^n + a_1D^{n-1} + \dots + a_n)y = q(x) \implies P(D)y = q(x) $$

Homogeneous Linear Differential Equations of Second Order

The Characteristic Equation

Standard form: $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + a\frac{dy}{dx} + by = 0$.
Consider the trial solution $y = e^{mx}$. Substituting $y'=me^{mx}$ and $y''=m^2e^{mx}$ gives:
$$ e^{mx}(m^2 + am + b) = 0 $$
Since $e^{mx} \neq 0$, we get the Auxiliary (or Characteristic) Equation:
$$ m^2 + am + b = 0 $$
The general solution depends entirely on the nature of the roots $m_1, m_2$ of this quadratic equation.
Nature of Roots and Solutions

  • Case I: Two distinct real roots ($m_1 \neq m_2$)
    $y = Ae^{m_1x} + Be^{m_2x}$
  • Case II: Two equal real roots ($m_1 = m_2 = m$)
    To maintain linear independence, we multiply the second term by $x$:
    $y = (Ax + B)e^{mx}$
  • Case III: Complex conjugate roots ($m = p \pm iq$)
    Using Euler's formula, the solution reduces to trigonometric terms:
    $y = e^{px}(A\cos qx + B\sin qx)$

Worked Example (Case II): Solve $y'' - 6y' + 9y = 0$.
1. Characteristic Eq: $m^2 - 6m + 9 = 0$
2. Factor: $(m - 3)^2 = 0 \implies m = 3$ (repeated root).
3. General Solution: $y = (Ax + B)e^{3x}$.

Nonhomogeneous Equations & Undetermined Coefficients

The general solution of the second order nonhomogeneous linear equation $y'' + p_1(x)y' + p_2(x)y = f(x)$ is $y = y_c + y_p$. The term $y_c$ solves the corresponding homogeneous equation $=0$, and $y_p$ is any specific function satisfying the nonhomogeneous equation.
Method of Undetermined Coefficients

We find $y_p(x)$ by substituting a "guessed" form based on the nature of $f(x)$:
Form of $f(x)$ Initial Guess for $y_p$
Constant $k$ $c$
Linear $kx$ $ax + b$
Quadratic $kx^2$ $ax^2 + bx + c$
Trigonometric $k\sin x$ or $k\cos x$ $a\sin x + b\cos x$
Exponential $e^{kx}$ $ce^{kx}$ (if $k$ is not a root of aux eq)
Problem Solving Steps

  1. Solve the complementary equation for $y_c$.
  2. Make an initial guess for $y_p(x)$ based on $f(x)$.
  3. Crucial Step: Check if any term in $y_p$ overlaps with $y_c$. If so, multiply the guess by $x$ (or $x^2$) until there is no overlap.
  4. Substitute $y_p(x)$ into the ODE and equate coefficients to find unknown constants.
  5. Add $y = y_c + y_p$.
