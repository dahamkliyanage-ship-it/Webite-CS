Vectors

Graphical Representation

In applied mathematics and engineering mechanics, distinguishing between scalars and vectors is essential. A scalar (like mass, temperature, or time) only has a size. A vector must have both a size and a specific direction.

Definition: A vector is a quantity having both magnitude and direction. It is represented by an arrow, the direction of which indicates the direction of the vector and the length of which is the magnitude.

Figure 1:

The vector \(AB\) has,

  • a magnitude – length of \(AB\) and
  • a direction – given by the arrow

Geometrically a vector is represented by a directed line segment such as \(\vec{AB}\) where the magnitude of the vector is the length of the line segment and the direction of the vector is shown by an arrow sign.

Practical Example: Think of a car moving at 50 km/h towards the East. The speed (50 km/h) is the magnitude, and "East" is the direction. If you only say "50 km/h", that is just a scalar (speed). Saying "50 km/h East" makes it a vector (velocity).

Note. Notations such as \(\vec{PQ}\) or \(\underline{a}\) are used for a vector and the magnitude of the vector is denoted by \(|\vec{PQ}|\) or \(|\underline{a}|\).
Definition: Two vectors are equal if they have the same length and are parallel having the same direction.

Tip: Two vectors can be equal even if they start at different points. For instance, two parallel train tracks pointing North with trains moving at the exact same speed represent equal vectors.


Scalar Multiplication and Addition of vectors

Definition: Let \(\underline{a}\) be a vector and \(\lambda\) be a positive real number. Then the vector whose magnitude is \(\lambda|\underline{a}|\) and the direction is the same as that of \(\underline{a}\) is called a scalar multiple of \(\underline{a}\) and is denoted by \(\lambda\underline{a}\).

Figure 2:

Example of Scalar Multiplication: If \(\underline{a}\) represents a structural force of 10 Newtons acting downwards on an elastic beam, then \(3\underline{a}\) represents a force of 30 Newtons acting in that exact same downward direction. The magnitude is tripled, but the line of action stays the same.

Definition(Unit Vector): A unit vector is a vector with a magnitude is 1 unit.

The unit vector along the vector \(\underline{a}\) is given by \(\frac{\underline{a}}{|\underline{a}|}\).

Similarly, \(\frac{\vec{PQ}}{|\vec{PQ}|}\) represents a unit vector along the vector \(\vec{PQ}\).

Unit vectors are incredibly useful because they isolate the direction of a vector without interfering with the magnitude when used in calculations.

Definition: Suppose \(\underline{a}\) and \(\underline{b}\) are two vectors represented by \(\vec{OA}\) and \(\vec{AB}\) respectively. Then the vector \(\vec{OB}\) is called the sum of \(\vec{OA}\) and \(\vec{AB}\).

Figure 3:

This is called the triangle law.

\(\vec{OB} = \vec{OA} + \vec{AB} = \underline{a} + \underline{b}\)

Example of Addition: Imagine a particle's displacement. If you walk from point O to point A, and then from point A to point B, your net displacement (your final distance and direction from where you started) is exactly the same as if you had walked straight from O to B.


Vectors in a Plane

Consider the \(XY\) plane. \(\underline{i}\) and \(\underline{j}\) are the unit vectors along the directions \(OX\) (horizontal) and \(OY\) (vertical) respectively. These form the fundamental building blocks of coordinate geometry.

Definition: If \(P = (a_1, a_2)\) then \(\vec{OP} = a_1\underline{i} + a_2\underline{j}\) and \(\vec{OP}\) is called the position vector of \(P\) with respect to the origin \(O\). \(a_1\) and \(a_2\) are the components of \(\vec{OP}\). (See figure 4.)

Figure 4:

Applying the Concept: If a point \(P\) has coordinates \((3, 4)\), its position vector is \(\vec{OP} = 3\underline{i} + 4\underline{j}\). This simply means you move 3 units horizontally along the x-axis and 4 units vertically along the y-axis to reach point \(P\).

We can easily calculate the exact length (magnitude) of this vector using Pythagoras' theorem:

\(|\vec{OP}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5\) units.
