Vectors in 3-Space

$\underline{i}$, $\underline{j}$ and $\underline{k}$ are the unit vectors from the origin along the axes $X, Y$, and $Z$ respectively.

Definition: Let $\langle a_1, a_2, a_3 \rangle$ be the position vector of $P$ with respect to the origin $O$. (See figure 5.)

Figure 5

Then $\overrightarrow{OP} = a_1\underline{i} + a_2\underline{j} + a_3\underline{k}$ and $|\overrightarrow{OP}| = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2}$.

According to the figure,

$$ \overrightarrow{OP} = |\overrightarrow{OP}|(\cos\alpha\underline{i} + \cos\beta\underline{j} + \cos\gamma\underline{k}) \text{ and } \frac{\overrightarrow{OP}}{|\overrightarrow{OP}|} = \cos\alpha\underline{i} + \cos\beta\underline{j} + \cos\gamma\underline{k} $$

where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are the angles between $OP$ and coordinate axes $X, Y$, and $Z$ respectively.

Properties of Direction Angles

It can be shown that
$$ \cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma = 1 $$

Moreover, $\frac{\overrightarrow{OP}}{|\overrightarrow{OP}|}$ is the unit vector along the direction of $\overrightarrow{OP}$.

  • $\cos\alpha, \cos\beta, \cos\gamma$ are called the direction cosines of the vector $\overrightarrow{OP}$.
  • $\cos\alpha : \cos\beta : \cos\gamma$ is called the direction ratio of the vector $\overrightarrow{OP}$.
Engineering Mechanics Example: Analyzing a 3D Force Vector

When determining the resultant forces acting on a spatial joint in structural mechanics, vectors are broken down into their 3D components.

Suppose a force vector acting on a structural node is given by $\overrightarrow{F} = 20\underline{i} + 30\underline{j} - 60\underline{k}$ Newtons.

1. Calculate the Magnitude:
$$ |\overrightarrow{F}| = \sqrt{20^2 + 30^2 + (-60)^2} = \sqrt{400 + 900 + 3600} = \sqrt{4900} = 70 \text{ N} $$
2. Determine the Direction Cosines:
By dividing each component by the overall magnitude, we find the cosines of the angles the force makes with the $X, Y$, and $Z$ axes respectively:
  • $\cos\alpha = \frac{20}{70} = \frac{2}{7}$
  • $\cos\beta = \frac{30}{70} = \frac{3}{7}$
  • $\cos\gamma = \frac{-60}{70} = -\frac{6}{7}$
3. Verification of Identity:
We can rigorously verify the fundamental mathematical property of the direction cosines:
$$ \left(\frac{2}{7}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{3}{7}\right)^2 + \left(-\frac{6}{7}\right)^2 = \frac{4}{49} + \frac{9}{49} + \frac{36}{49} = \frac{49}{49} = 1 $$
This confirms the unit vector identity holds perfectly along the line of action of the structural force.
