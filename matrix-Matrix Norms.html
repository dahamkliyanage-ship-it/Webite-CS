Matrix Norms

Let $A$ be an arbitrary $m \times n$ matrix with real entries. A norm of $A$ is a non-negative real number denoted by $||A||$. It acts as a measure of the "magnitude" or "size" of the matrix. There are several different ways of defining a norm, some of which are provided below.

Definition: For a matrix $A = (a_{ij})_{m \times n}$, the 1-norm is denoted by $||A||_1$ and defined by
$$ ||A||_1 = \max_{1 \le j \le n} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{m} |a_{ij}| \right). $$
That is, the 1-norm is the maximum of the absolute column sums.
Definition: For a matrix $A = (a_{ij})_{m \times n}$, the Euclidean norm or 2-norm is denoted by $||A||_E$ or $||A||_2$ and defined by
$$ ||A||_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} (a_{ij})^2}. $$
That is, the 2-norm (also known as the Frobenius norm) is the square root of the sum of all the squared elements of the matrix.
Definition: For a matrix $A = (a_{ij})_{m \times n}$, the infinity norm is denoted by $||A||_\infty$ and defined by
$$ ||A||_\infty = \max_{1 \le i \le m} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{n} |a_{ij}| \right). $$
That is, the infinity norm is the maximum of the absolute row sums.
Worked Example: Calculating Matrix Norms

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$. Find the 1-norm, infinity norm, and Euclidean norm.

1. Finding $||A||_1$ (Max Column Sum):
Sum of absolute values for Column 1: $|1| + |3| = 4$
Sum of absolute values for Column 2: $|-2| + |4| = 6$
$$ ||A||_1 = \max(4, 6) = 6 $$
2. Finding $||A||_\infty$ (Max Row Sum):
Sum of absolute values for Row 1: $|1| + |-2| = 3$
Sum of absolute values for Row 2: $|3| + |4| = 7$
$$ ||A||_\infty = \max(3, 7) = 7 $$
3. Finding $||A||_2$ (Euclidean/Frobenius Norm):
Square every element, sum them, and take the square root:
$$ ||A||_2 = \sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2 + 3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 9 + 16} = \sqrt{30} \approx 5.477 $$
Definition: A norm defined for column vectors is called a vector norm.

Properties of Norms

Any matrix norm (or a vector norm) satisfies the properties described in above theorem

Theorem: For given $m \times n$ matrices $A$ and $B$ and a norm $|| \cdot ||$:
  1. $||A|| \ge 0$. (Norms are non-negative)
  2. $||A|| = 0$ if and only if $A = 0$. (Only the zero matrix has a zero norm)
  3. $||kA|| = |k| ||A||$ where $k$ is a scalar. (Absolute scalability)
  4. $||A + B|| \le ||A|| + ||B||$. (The Triangle Inequality)
  5. $||AB|| \le ||A|| ||B||$. (Sub-multiplicativity, provided the matrix product $AB$ is defined)
