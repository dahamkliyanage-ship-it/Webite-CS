Topic 05

Allotropy

Allotropy is the remarkable ability of a single substance to exist in more than one physical form depending on temperature or pressure[cite: 12, 13].

For example, Iron changes its fundamental structure when heated[cite: 12]:

  • Below 910°C: Iron has a BCC structure (α-iron)
  • Between 910°C and 1400°C: Iron transitions to an FCC structure (γ-iron)
  • Above 1400°C: Iron transitions back to a BCC structure (δ-iron)

Another classic example is Carbon, which can form drastically different allotropes including Diamond, Graphite, Fullerenes, and Carbon Nanotubes (a single sheet of graphite rolled into a tube, capped with fullerene hemispheres)[cite: 12].
