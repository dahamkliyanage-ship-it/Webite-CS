Adjoint and Inverse

Adjoint of a matrix

Definition: Suppose $A = (a_{ij})_{n \times n}$ is a square matrix. Then the transpose of the matrix $(A_{ij})_{n \times n}$ where $A_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}|M_{ij}|$ is called the adjoint of the matrix $A$ and is denoted by $\text{adj } A$.

Note. The adjoint is the transpose of the co-factor matrix.
Example 1. Find $\text{adj } A$ if $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 3 & 4 \\ -2 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$.

Solution: First, we compute all individual co-factors $A_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}|M_{ij}|$:
$$ A_{11} = \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 \end{vmatrix} = 18 - 20 = -2 $$ $$ A_{12} = -\begin{vmatrix} -1 & 4 \\ -2 & 6 \end{vmatrix} = -(-6 - (-8)) = -2 $$ $$ A_{13} = \begin{vmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ -2 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = -5 - (-6) = 1 $$ $$ A_{21} = -\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 5 & 6 \end{vmatrix} = -(12 - 0) = -12 $$ $$ A_{22} = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 6 \end{vmatrix} = 6 - 0 = 6 $$ $$ A_{23} = -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = -(5 - (-4)) = -9 $$ $$ A_{31} = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 3 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = 8 - 0 = 8 $$ $$ A_{32} = -\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 4 \end{vmatrix} = -(4 - 0) = -4 $$ $$ A_{33} = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ -1 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = 3 - (-2) = 5 $$
Observe that:
$$ \text{adj } A = \begin{pmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} & A_{13} \\ A_{21} & A_{22} & A_{23} \\ A_{31} & A_{32} & A_{33} \end{pmatrix}^T = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -2 & 1 \\ -12 & 6 & -9 \\ 8 & -4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}^T = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -12 & 8 \\ -2 & 6 & -4 \\ 1 & -9 & 5 \end{pmatrix} $$

6.2 Inverse of a matrix

Definition: Suppose $A$ and $B$ are square matrices of the same order. If $AB = BA = I$ then $B$ is called the inverse of $A$ and is denoted by $A^{-1}$. That is $B = A^{-1}$.

Note. If $B$ is the inverse of $A$ then $A$ is the inverse of $B$.
Theorem: Suppose $A$ is a square matrix. If $|A| \neq 0$ then
$$ A^{-1} = \frac{\text{adj } A}{|A|} $$
Important Classification Notes:
  • A matrix $A$ is said to be singular if $|A| = 0$. In this case, the inverse $A^{-1}$ does not exist.
  • Otherwise, if $|A| \neq 0$, it is called non-singular. Non-singular matrices are also called invertible matrices.
