Introduction

The Riemann Integral Definition

Let $f: [a, b] \to \mathbb{R}$ be a bounded (not necessarily continuous) function on a compact (closed, bounded) interval.

We will define what it means for $f$ to be Riemann integrable on $[a, b]$ and, in that case, we define its Riemann integral as:
$$ \int_a^b f. $$
Note: The requirement that the interval be compact is crucial, as improper integrals are required if the interval extends to infinity or if the function is unbounded.
Geometric Interpretation & Types of Integrals

  • Definite Integral: The integral of $f$ on $[a, b]$ is a real number whose geometrical interpretation is the signed area under the graph $y = f(x)$ for $a \le x \le b$. This number is also called the definite integral of $f$.
  • Indefinite Integral: By integrating $f$ over an interval $[a, x]$ with varying right end-point, we get a function of $x$, called the indefinite integral of $f$.
The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

The most important result about integration is the fundamental theorem of calculus, which states that integration and differentiation are inverse operations in an appropriately understood sense.

Among other things, this connection enables us to compute many integrals explicitly. If $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$ (meaning $F'(x) = f(x)$), then the theorem states:
$$ \int_a^b f(x) \, dx = F(b) - F(a) $$
Added Example: Applying the Fundamental Theorem

Evaluate the definite integral of the function $f(x) = 3x^2$ on the interval $[1, 3]$.

Step 1: Find the indefinite integral.
We need to find a function $F(x)$ such that $F'(x) = 3x^2$. Using basic power rules for anti-derivatives, we get $F(x) = x^3$.

Step 2: Apply the limits of integration.
Following the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:
$$ \int_1^3 3x^2 \, dx = F(3) - F(1) $$ $$ = (3)^3 - (1)^3 $$ $$ = 27 - 1 = 26 $$
The signed area under the curve $y = 3x^2$ from $x = 1$ to $x = 3$ is exactly 26.
