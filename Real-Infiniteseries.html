Introduction to Infinite Series

In finite mathematics, addition is associative and commutative: you can group or reorder numbers however you like, and the total never changes[cite: 377]. With infinite series, that intuition breaks down[cite: 377]. Adding terms indefinitely is not simple addition; it is the limit of a running total (partial sums)[cite: 377].

If you blindly add an infinite sequence without verifying convergence, you can "prove" that $1-1+1-1+\cdot\cdot\cdot=1/2$ or that $1+2+3+\cdot\cdot\cdot=-1/12$[cite: 377]. Convergence tests are safety criteria that tell you whether an infinite sum settles to a stable, finite value before you try to compute with it[cite: 377].

1. Infinite Series & Basic Comparison Tests

Definitions & Key Theorems[cite: 377, 378]

  • Definition (Series): A series is a sequence of partial sums $S_{n}=\Sigma_{k=1}^{n}u_{k},$ where $u_{k}$ is a sequence of terms[cite: 377].
  • Infinite Sum: The infinite sum is defined as the limit of partial sums[cite: 377]. If $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}S_{n}=S\in\mathbb{R},$ the series converges to S[cite: 377]. Otherwise, it diverges[cite: 377].

  • If $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{n}$ converges, then $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}$ converges[cite: 377].
  • If $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}$ diverges, then $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}v_{n}$ diverges[cite: 377].
  • Limit Comparison Test: Let $u_n, v_{n}>0$ and $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{u_{n}}{v_{n}}=R$[cite: 377].
    • If $R\in(0,\infty)$, then $\sum u_{n}$ converges $\Leftrightarrow\Sigma~v_{n}$ converges[cite: 377].
    • If $R=0$ and $\Sigma v_{n}$ converges, then $\sum\mathfrak{u}_{n}$ converges[cite: 377].
    • If $R=\infty$ and $\Sigma v_{n}$ diverges, then $\sum u_{n}$ diverges[cite: 378].

    Exercise 1.1: Geometric Series
    Prove that the geometric series $\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{k}$ converges $\Leftrightarrow|r|<1$, and find its sum[cite: 377].
    Partial Sum Formula: $S_{n}=r+r^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+r^{n}=\frac{r(1-r^{n})}{1-r}(r\ne1)$[cite: 378].
    If $|r|<1$, $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r^{n}=0$[cite: 378]. Thus, $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}S_{n}=\frac{r}{1-r}$[cite: 378].
    If $|r|\ge1$, $r^n$ does not approach 0, so the series diverges[cite: 378].

    Exercise 1.2: Direct Comparison Test
    Check convergence of $\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{k^{2}+3}$[cite: 378].
    For all $k\ge1$, $\frac{2}{k^{2}+3}<\frac{2}{k^{2}}$[cite: 378]. We know the p-series $\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}}$ converges $(p=2>1)$[cite: 378]. Therefore, by the Direct Comparison Test, it converges[cite: 378].

    2. Integral, Ratio & Root Tests

    Definitions & Key Theorems[cite: 378, 379]

    • Integral Test: Let $u:[1,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)$ be a continuous, decreasing function[cite: 378]. $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty} u(n)$ converges $\Leftrightarrow\int_{1}^{\infty}u(x)dx$ converges[cite: 378].
    • p-Series Theorem: $\zeta(p)=\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{p}}$ converges $\Leftrightarrow p>1$[cite: 379].
    • Ratio Test: Let $u_{n}>0$ and $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_{n}}=R$[cite: 379].
      • If $R<1,$ $\Sigma u_{n}$ converges[cite: 379].
      • If $R>1,\Sigma u_{n}$ diverges[cite: 379].
      • If $R=1$, the test is inconclusive[cite: 379].
    • Root Test: Let $u_{n}>0$ and $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(u_{n})^{1/n}=R$[cite: 379]. Rules for $R$ match the Ratio Test[cite: 379].
    Worked Exercises[cite: 379, 380]

    Exercise 2.2: Apply the Ratio Test
    Test convergence of $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(2n)!}$[cite: 379].
    Compute the ratio:
    $$ \frac{u_{n+1}}{u_{n}} = \frac{(n+1)^{2}}{(2n+2)(2n+1)} = \frac{n+1}{2(2n+1)} $$
    Take the limit as $n\rightarrow\infty$: $R = \frac{1}{4}$[cite: 380]. Since $R < 1$, the series converges[cite: 380].

    Exercise 2.3: Apply the Root Test
    Test convergence of $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}(\frac{n}{n+1})^{n^{2}}$[cite: 380].
    Compute the root:
    $$ (u_{n})^{1/n} = \left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n} = \frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}} $$
    Take the limit as $n\rightarrow\infty$: $R = \frac{1}{e}$[cite: 380]. Since $1/e < 1$, the series converges[cite: 380].

    3. Alternating Series, Absolute vs. Conditional Convergence

    Definitions & Key Theorems[cite: 380, 381]

    • Absolute Convergence: $\Sigma u_{n}$ is absolutely convergent iff $\Sigma|u_{n}|$ converges[cite: 380]. Absolute convergence implies convergence[cite: 381].
    • Conditional Convergence: If $\Sigma u_{n}$ converges, but $\Sigma|u_{n}|$ diverges, the series is conditionally convergent[cite: 380].
    • Leibniz Alternating Series Test: Let $\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}u_{n}$ with $u_{n}>0$[cite: 381]. If $u_{n}$ is decreasing and $\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n}=0$, then the series converges[cite: 381].
    • Raabe's Test: When the Ratio Test fails ($R=1$), evaluate $L=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n(\frac{u_{n}}{u_{n+1}}-1)$[cite: 381]. If $L>1$, it converges; if $L<1$, it diverges[cite: 381].
