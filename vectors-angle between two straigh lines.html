The Angle Between Two Straight Lines

Defining the Intersecting Lines

Consider the two intersecting lines $\alpha : \frac{x - x_1}{a_1} = \frac{y - y_1}{b_1} = \frac{z - z_1}{c_1}$ and $\beta : \frac{x - x_2}{a_2} = \frac{y - y_2}{b_2} = \frac{z - z_2}{c_2}$ in the 3-space.

Clearly, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ pass through the points $(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2, z_2)$ and are parallel to the vectors $\langle a_1, b_1, c_1 \rangle$ and $\langle a_2, b_2, c_2 \rangle$ respectively.

Geometric Insight: Because the orientation of any straight line is completely determined by its direction vector, the angle between any two lines in 3D space is identical to the angle between their respective direction vectors, provided we measure them from the same starting point (the origin).


Figure 3: Geometric relationship between the angle of intersecting lines and their direction vectors.

Deriving the Angle Formula

Theorem

Therefore, the angle between two lines is same as the angle between two vectors and
$$ \cos \phi = \frac{(a_1\underline{i} + b_1\underline{j} + c_1\underline{k}) \cdot (a_2\underline{i} + b_2\underline{j} + c_2\underline{k})}{|a_1\underline{i} + b_1\underline{j} + c_1\underline{k}||a_2\underline{i} + b_2\underline{j} + c_2\underline{k}|} = \frac{a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2 + c_1 c_2}{\sqrt{(a_1^2 + b_1^2 + c_1^2)(a_2^2 + b_2^2 + c_2^2)}} $$


Important Consequence: If the two lines are perpendicular (orthogonal), their angle is $90^\circ$. Since $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$, the numerator of our formula must equal zero. Thus, lines are perpendicular if and only if $a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2 + c_1 c_2 = 0$.

Detailed Mathematical Example

Example: Finding the Angle Between Two 3D Lines

Find the acute angle $\phi$ between the two lines defined by the symmetric equations:
Line 1 ($\alpha$): $\frac{x-1}{1} = \frac{y+2}{2} = \frac{z-3}{2}$
Line 2 ($\beta$): $\frac{x}{0} = \frac{y-1}{3} = \frac{z+2}{4}$

Step 1: Extract the direction vectors.
The denominators of the symmetric equations give us the direction vectors.
  • $\underline{v_1} = \langle a_1, b_1, c_1 \rangle = \langle 1, 2, 2 \rangle$
  • $\underline{v_2} = \langle a_2, b_2, c_2 \rangle = \langle 0, 3, 4 \rangle$
Step 2: Calculate the dot product (the numerator).
$$ \underline{v_1} \cdot \underline{v_2} = (1)(0) + (2)(3) + (2)(4) = 0 + 6 + 8 = 14 $$
Step 3: Calculate the magnitudes (the denominator components).
$$ |\underline{v_1}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = \sqrt{9} = 3 $$ $$ |\underline{v_2}| = \sqrt{0^2 + 3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{0 + 9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 $$
Step 4: Apply the angle formula.
$$ \cos \phi = \frac{14}{(3)(5)} = \frac{14}{15} $$ $$ \phi = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{14}{15}\right) \approx 21.04^\circ $$
The acute angle between the two lines is approximately $21.04^\circ$.
