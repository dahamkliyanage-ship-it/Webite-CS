Topic 01

Fundamentals of Conduction

An electric current results from the motion of electrically charged particles (typically electrons) responding to forces from an external electric field[cite: 11]. Electric Current (I) is the total charge (Q) passing through a given point per unit time (t), measured in Amperes (A)[cite: 11].

Ohm's Law & Conductivity

Ohm's Law dictates that Voltage (V) equals Current (I) multiplied by Resistance (R): V = I · R[cite: 11].

  • Electrical Resistivity (ρ): A fundamental measure of how strongly a material opposes the flow of electric current[cite: 11]. Calculated as R = ρ(l/A), where 'l' is length and 'A' is cross-sectional area. Measured in Ω·m[cite: 11].
  • Electrical Conductivity (σ): The reciprocal of resistivity (σ = 1/ρ). Measured in S/m or (Ω·m)-1[cite: 11].

Electron Mobility

In a solid, moving electrons scatter off atomic cores and imperfections, creating a net drift in the direction opposite to the electric field[cite: 11]. Drift Velocity (v̄) is the average velocity attained by these charge carriers[cite: 11]. The Carrier Mobility (μ) evaluates this drift relative to the electric field (μ = v̄ / E)[cite: 11].

The master formula for conductivity is: σ = n · q · μ, meaning conductivity can be improved by increasing the number of charge carriers (n) or their mobility (μ)[cite: 11].
