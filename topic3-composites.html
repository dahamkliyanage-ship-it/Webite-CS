Topic 03

Composites & Advanced Materials

Composites are formed by combining two or more distinct types of materials to create a new material whose properties are superior to those of the individual components. This allows engineers to perfectly tailor properties to suit specific applications.

  • Concrete: A composite mixture of cement, sand, aggregates, and admixtures.
  • Fiberglass: A composite of glass fiber and plastic.
  • Polymer/Ceramic Composites: These combinations yield a material with a greater Young's modulus than the base polymer, while remaining less brittle than the pure ceramic.

Beyond the primary classes, modern engineering categorizes advanced materials based on their high-tech applications:

  • Semiconductors: Materials that can be switched from an insulator (off) to a conductor (on) via the application of voltage.
  • Nanomaterials: Materials sized between 1 to 100 nm, where their geometry and size play a significant role in dictating their properties.
  • Biomaterials: Materials implanted into the human body (like a titanium hip joint). They must be biocompatible, meaning they have the ability to survive in the body without causing harm.
  • Smart Materials: Materials that, much like biological systems, inherently respond to external stimuli.
