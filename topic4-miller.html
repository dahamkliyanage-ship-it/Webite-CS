Topic 04

Miller Indices

Engineers use a coordinate system called Miller Indices to identify specific directions and planes within a crystal lattice[cite: 12, 13].

  • Directions: Indicated strictly by square brackets. The [100], [110], and [111] directions are common ones. A "family" of equivalent directions is indicated by angle brackets, e.g., <110>[cite: 12].
  • Planes: Indicated by parentheses, e.g., (100) or (111). To find the Miller Indices of a plane, you locate the intercepts of the plane on the x, y, and z axes, take their reciprocals, and clear any fractions to leave whole numbers[cite: 12].

When analyzing these planes, two important concepts arise[cite: 12]:

  • Close Packed Plane: A specific crystal plane where all neighboring atoms are touching each other (e.g., the (111) plane in an FCC structure)[cite: 12].
  • Planar Density (Aerial Density): The number of atoms packed into a specific unit area (usually 1 m2) of a crystal plane[cite: 12].
