Separable Equations

Separable ODEs

An ODE is said to be separable if the parts that depend on $x$ and $y$ can be separated into different sides of the equation. This makes it possible to integrate both sides separately.

An equation is separable if it can be written as:
$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y), $$
for some function $f(x)$ that only depends on $x$ but not on $y$, and some function $g(y)$ that only depends on $y$ and not on $x$. If we multiply through by $dx$ and divide by $g(y)$, the equation becomes:
$$ \frac{dy}{g(y)} = f(x)dx. $$
So we may integrate:
$$ \int \frac{dy}{g(y)} = \int f(x)dx. $$
Definition:

For real non-zero values of $x$, the exponential integral $Ei(x)$ is defined as:
$$ Ei(x) = -\int_{-x}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-t}}{t} dt $$
Detail: The exponential integral is a special function that frequently arises in physics and engineering applications when solving certain separable differential equations that cannot be evaluated using elementary functions.
Example 2.1

Find and illustrate the one-parameter family of solutions for the ODE:
$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y} $$
Detailed Solution:
1. Separate variables: Multiply both sides by $y$ and $dx$ to get $x$ and $y$ on opposite sides.
$$ y \, dy = -x \, dx $$
2. Integrate both sides:
$$ \int y \, dy = -\int x \, dx $$ $$ \frac{y^2}{2} = -\frac{x^2}{2} + C $$
3. Simplify: Multiply by 2 and rearrange.
$$ x^2 + y^2 = 2C $$
If we let $2C = R^2$, the solution is $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$, which represents a one-parameter family of concentric circles centered at the origin.

Homogeneous Equation

Definition:

Suppose
$$ \frac{dy}{dx} = f(x, y). $$
$f(x, y)$ is said to be homogeneous if
$$ f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = f(x, y) $$
for every non-zero real value $\lambda$.
Method of Solving

  • Determine whether the equation is homogeneous or not by applying the test $f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = f(x, y)$.
  • Use the substitution $y = vx$ and therefore its derivative $\frac{dy}{dx} = v + x\frac{dv}{dx}$ in the original differential equation.
  • Separate the variables $x$ and $v$. (The substitution guarantees the equation will become separable).
  • Integrate both sides.
  • Substitute $v = \frac{y}{x}$ back into the equation to get the general solution.
  • Use initial conditions (if given) to find the specific constant values.
Worked Example: Solving a Homogeneous ODE

Solve the differential equation: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{xy}$

Step 1: Check if homogeneous.
$f(\lambda x, \lambda y) = \frac{(\lambda x)^2 + (\lambda y)^2}{(\lambda x)(\lambda y)} = \frac{\lambda^2(x^2 + y^2)}{\lambda^2(xy)} = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{xy} = f(x,y)$. It is homogeneous.

Step 2: Substitute $y = vx$ and $y' = v + xv'$.
$$ v + x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{x^2 + (vx)^2}{x(vx)} = \frac{x^2(1 + v^2)}{x^2v} = \frac{1 + v^2}{v} = \frac{1}{v} + v $$
Step 3: Separate variables $x$ and $v$.
Subtract $v$ from both sides:
$$ x\frac{dv}{dx} = \frac{1}{v} $$ $$ v \, dv = \frac{1}{x} dx $$
Step 4: Integrate both sides.
$$ \int v \, dv = \int \frac{1}{x} dx $$ $$ \frac{v^2}{2} = \ln|x| + C $$
Step 5: Substitute back $v = y/x$.
$$ \frac{(y/x)^2}{2} = \ln|x| + C \implies y^2 = 2x^2(\ln|x| + C) $$
