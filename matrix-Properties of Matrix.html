Properties of Matrix Multiplication and Matrix Transpose

Theorem: For three matrices $A$, $B$, $C$,

$(i). \quad A(BC) = (AB)C$

$(ii). \quad (A + B)C = AC + BC$

$(iii). \quad A(B + C) = AB + AC$

$(iv). \quad AI = IA = A \text{ where } I \text{ is an identity matrix.}$

Note. In each part of above theorem, the matrices $A$, $B$, $C$ should be chosen so that the mentioned product matrices are defined.
Definition:

  • A square matrix is said to be symmetric if it is equal to its transpose. That is $A = A^T$.
  • A square matrix is said to be skew-symmetric if it is equal to the negative of its transpose. That is $A = -A^T$.
Theorem: For two matrices $A$ and $B$ and a scalar $k$,

$(i). \quad (A^T)^T = A$

$(ii). \quad (A + B)^T = A^T + B^T$

$(iii). \quad (kA)^T = kA^T$

$(iv). \quad (AB)^T = B^T A^T$
Worked Example: Verifying Transpose Properties

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$. Verify that $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$.

Step 1: Compute product $AB$.
$$ AB = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} (1)(0)+(2)(2) & (1)(1)+(2)(5) \\ (3)(0)+(4)(2) & (3)(1)+(4)(5) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 11 \\ 8 & 23 \end{pmatrix} $$
Step 2: Transpose the product $(AB)^T$.
$$ (AB)^T = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 8 \\ 11 & 23 \end{pmatrix} $$
Step 3: Compute $B^T A^T$ and compare.
$$ B^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}, \quad A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} $$ $$ B^T A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} (0)(1)+(2)(2) & (0)(3)+(2)(4) \\ (1)(1)+(5)(2) & (1)(3)+(5)(4) \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 8 \\ 11 & 23 \end{pmatrix} $$
Thus, $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$ is successfully verified.
