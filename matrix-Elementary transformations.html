Elementary transformations (row and column operations) on matrices

Definition:

The following three types of matrix transformations are known as elementary row and column operations or elementary transformations:

  1. Interchange of any columns or rows.
  2. Addition to any column or row a multiple of any other column or row.
  3. Multiplication of each element of a column or a row by a non-zero constant.

If the matrix $B$ is obtained by applying elementary row or column operations to $A$ then the matrix $A$ is equivalent to $B$, which is denoted by $A \approx B$.
Theorem: Suppose $A$ is a square matrix. The elementary row operations that reduce the matrix $A$ to the identity matrix transform the identity matrix to the inverse of $A$.
Example 2. Use the elementary row operations to find the inverse of $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.

Solution (Augmented Matrix Method): We form the augmented matrix $[A \mid I]$ and apply row operations until we get $[I \mid A^{-1}]$:
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 & 4 & \mid & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 & \mid & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 & \mid & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 & \mid & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 & \mid & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 & 4 & \mid & 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} $$ $$ \longrightarrow \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 & \mid & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 & -15 & \mid & 0 & 1 & -4 \\ 0 & -1 & -4 & \mid & 1 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 & \mid & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & \mid & 0 & -1/5 & 4/5 \\ 0 & -1 & -4 & \mid & 1 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix} $$ $$ \longrightarrow \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 & \mid & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & \mid & 0 & -1/5 & 4/5 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 & \mid & 1 & -1/5 & -6/5 \end{pmatrix} \longrightarrow \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -2 & \mid & 0 & 2/5 & -3/5 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & \mid & 0 & -1/5 & 4/5 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \mid & -1 & 1/5 & 6/5 \end{pmatrix} $$ $$ \longrightarrow \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & \mid & -2 & 4/5 & 9/5 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & \mid & 3 & -4/5 & -14/5 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & \mid & -1 & 1/5 & 6/5 \end{pmatrix} $$
Therefore, the inverse matrix is:
$$ \therefore A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 4/5 & 9/5 \\ 3 & -4/5 & -14/5 \\ -1 & 1/5 & 6/5 \end{pmatrix} $$
