Scalar Triple Product of Vectors

Definition:
  • Suppose $\underline{a}$, $\underline{b}$, and $\underline{c}$ are three vectors. Then the scalar triple product of $\underline{a}$, $\underline{b}$, and $\underline{c}$ is defined by taking the dot product of the first vector with the cross product of the other two: $\underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c})$. It is commonly denoted by $[\underline{a}, \underline{b}, \underline{c}]$.
  • If $\underline{a} = a_1\underline{i} + a_2\underline{j} + a_3\underline{k}$, $\underline{b} = b_1\underline{i} + b_2\underline{j} + b_3\underline{k}$, and $\underline{c} = c_1\underline{i} + c_2\underline{j} + c_3\underline{k}$, then the product can be computed rapidly using a $3 \times 3$ determinant:
$$ \underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} $$

Insight: The result of a scalar triple product is always a single real number (a scalar), not a vector. This happens because evaluating the cross product $(\underline{b} \times \underline{c})$ yields a vector, and taking the dot product of $\underline{a}$ with this new vector yields a scalar.

Detailed Example 1: Evaluating a Scalar Triple Product

Let $\underline{a} = 2\underline{i} + 1\underline{j} + 0\underline{k}$, $\underline{b} = 1\underline{i} - 1\underline{j} + 3\underline{k}$, and $\underline{c} = 0\underline{i} + 1\underline{j} + 2\underline{k}$. Find the scalar triple product $[\underline{a}, \underline{b}, \underline{c}]$.

Set up the determinant using the components of the three vectors in the exact order they are listed:
$$ \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} $$
Expand along the first row (the components of $\underline{a}$):
$$ = 2 \begin{vmatrix} -1 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} - 1 \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 2 \end{vmatrix} + 0 \begin{vmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix} $$ $$ = 2((-1)(2) - (3)(1)) - 1((1)(2) - (3)(0)) + 0 $$ $$ = 2(-2 - 3) - 1(2 - 0) $$ $$ = 2(-5) - 2 = -10 - 2 = -12 $$
The scalar triple product evaluates to $-12$.

Properties & Coplanarity

Definition: Vectors that lie perfectly flat on the same plane (or can be shifted to lie on the same plane) in a three-dimensional space are called coplanar vectors.
Theorem: For any given three vectors $\underline{a}$, $\underline{b}$, and $\underline{c}$:
  • Dot/Cross Interchange: $\underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c}) = (\underline{a} \times \underline{b}) \cdot \underline{c}$. The operations can be swapped without changing the result.
  • Cyclic Permutation: $\underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c}) = \underline{b} \cdot (\underline{c} \times \underline{a}) = \underline{c} \cdot (\underline{a} \times \underline{b})$. Shifting the vectors in a cycle preserves the value because shifting rows twice in a determinant keeps the sign identical.
  • Anti-Cyclic Permutation: Swapping any two adjacent vectors introduces a negative sign, e.g., $[\underline{a}, \underline{b}, \underline{c}] = -[\underline{a}, \underline{c}, \underline{b}]$.
  • Coplanarity Test: If $\underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c}) = 0$, then the vectors $\underline{a}$, $\underline{b}$, and $\underline{c}$ are absolutely coplanar.
Detailed Example 2: Checking for Coplanarity

Determine mathematically if the vectors $\underline{u} = \underline{i} + 2\underline{j} - \underline{k}$, $\underline{v} = 3\underline{i} + \underline{j} + 4\underline{k}$, and $\underline{w} = \underline{i} - 3\underline{j} + 6\underline{k}$ lie on the same plane.

We compute the scalar triple product. If the result is exactly $0$, they are coplanar.
$$ [\underline{u}, \underline{v}, \underline{w}] = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 3 & 1 & 4 \\ 1 & -3 & 6 \end{vmatrix} $$
$$ = 1 \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ -3 & 6 \end{vmatrix} - 2 \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 6 \end{vmatrix} + (-1) \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & -3 \end{vmatrix} $$ $$ = 1(6 - (-12)) - 2(18 - 4) - 1(-9 - 1) $$ $$ = 1(18) - 2(14) - 1(-10) = 18 - 28 + 10 = 0 $$
Because the scalar triple product equals $0$, we have proven that vectors $\underline{u}$, $\underline{v}$, and $\underline{w}$ are strictly coplanar.

Geometrical Applications

The scalar triple product is not just an abstract number; it represents physical 3D space. Specifically, it calculates the volume of the geometric shapes formed when you place three vectors tail-to-tail.


Figure 2: Parallelepiped Volume formed by three adjacent vectors
Volume Formulas
  • The volume of the parallelepiped with $\underline{a}$, $\underline{b}$, and $\underline{c}$ as adjacent edges is calculated as:
    $V_{\text{parallelepiped}} = |\underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c})|$
    (Absolute value is used because volume must always be a positive physical quantity).
  • The volume of the tetrahedron (a triangular pyramid) with $\underline{a}$, $\underline{b}$, and $\underline{c}$ as adjacent edges is exactly one-sixth of the parallelepiped:
    $V_{\text{tetrahedron}} = \frac{1}{6} |\underline{a} \cdot (\underline{b} \times \underline{c})|$
Detailed Example 3: Finding the Volume of a Tetrahedron from Coordinates

Suppose you are given four points in 3D space: $A(1,0,1)$, $B(2,3,0)$, $C(-1,1,4)$, and $D(0,3,2)$. Find the volume of the tetrahedron formed by these vertices.

Step 1: Form three vectors originating from the same point (let's use $A$):
  • $\underline{AB} = (2-1)\underline{i} + (3-0)\underline{j} + (0-1)\underline{k} = \langle 1, 3, -1 \rangle$
  • $\underline{AC} = (-1-1)\underline{i} + (1-0)\underline{j} + (4-1)\underline{k} = \langle -2, 1, 3 \rangle$
  • $\underline{AD} = (0-1)\underline{i} + (3-0)\underline{j} + (2-1)\underline{k} = \langle -1, 3, 1 \rangle$

Step 2: Compute the scalar triple product $[\underline{AB}, \underline{AC}, \underline{AD}]$:
$$ \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 3 & -1 \\ -2 & 1 & 3 \\ -1 & 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = 1(1 - 9) - 3(-2 - (-3)) + (-1)(-6 - (-1)) $$ $$ = 1(-8) - 3(1) - 1(-5) = -8 - 3 + 5 = -6 $$

Step 3: Apply the tetrahedron volume formula:
$$ V = \frac{1}{6} |-6| = \frac{1}{6}(6) = 1 \text{ cubic unit} $$
