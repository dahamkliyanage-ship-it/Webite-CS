Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Foundational Concepts

  • In some cases having $n$ linear equations in $n$ independent variables, it is required to find a solution vector $X$ proportional to $AX$, where $A$ is the coefficient matrix of the system.
  • This means $X$ should be a solution of the matrix equation $AX = \lambda X$ where $\lambda$ is a constant.
  • Rearranging this equation yields the homogeneous system $(A - \lambda I)X = O$.
  • Non-zero solutions exist for the system if the determinant $|A - \lambda I| = 0$, which gives different values of $\lambda$.
  • The numbers $\lambda_i$ for which non-zero solutions $X_i$ exist are called the eigenvalues of $A$, and the corresponding vectors $X_i$ are called the eigenvectors of $A$.
Definitions

  • Definition: If $A$ is an $n \times n$ matrix, then the polynomial of degree $n$ defined by $p(\lambda) = |A - \lambda I|$ is called the characteristic polynomial of $A$. The roots of the equation $p(\lambda) = 0$ are the eigenvalues, and the column vectors $X_1, X_2, \cdots, X_n$ satisfying $(A - \lambda_i I)X_i = O$ are the eigenvectors.
  • Definition: If the characteristic polynomial consists of a factor of the form $(\lambda - \lambda_i)^r$, which means eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ is repeated $r$ times, and $(\lambda - \lambda_i)^{r+1}$ is not a factor, then $r$ is called the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue $\lambda_i$.
  • Definition: The set of all eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \cdots, \lambda_n$ of $A$ is called the spectrum of $A$, and $R = \max\{|\lambda_1|, |\lambda_2|, \cdots, |\lambda_n|\}$ is called the spectral radius of $A$.

Linear Independence of Eigenvectors

Linear Independence

  • Definition: Suppose $X_1, X_2, \cdots, X_n$ is a set of $n$ vectors and $k_1, k_2, \cdots, k_n$ is a set of $n$ scalars. If $k_1X_1 + k_2X_2 + \cdots + k_nX_n = O \implies k_1 = k_2 = \cdots = k_n = 0$, then the vectors are linearly independent.
  • Theorem: Suppose the eigenvectors $X_1, X_2, \cdots, X_m$ correspond to $m$ distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \cdots, \lambda_m$ of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$. If they are linearly independent and $m = n$, then $X_1, X_2, \cdots, X_n$ forms a basis for the $n$-dimensional vector space associated with $A$.

Normalization of Eigenvectors

Scaling Vectors

  • Observe that a non-zero scalar multiple of an eigenvector remains an eigenvector of the equation $AX = \lambda X$.
  • This property is used to scale eigenvectors of $A$ to produce what we call normalized eigenvectors.
  • To normalize the eigenvector, we multiply it by the reciprocal of the square root of the sum of the squares of its components.
  • That operation makes the magnitude of the eigenvector equal to $1$.
Detailed Example & Norm Definition

  • For example, consider the eigenvector $X = \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}$.
  • The normalizing factor is $k = \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}$.
  • The normalized vector is evaluated as $\begin{pmatrix} \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}} \\ \frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}} \\ \frac{c}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}} \end{pmatrix}$.
  • It is easy to see that the magnitude of the normalized vector is $1$.
  • Definition: The norm of a row or column vector $W$ with real components $w_1, w_2, \cdots, w_n$ is denoted by $||W||$ and defined by $||W|| = \sqrt{w_1^2 + w_2^2 + \cdots + w_n^2}$.
  • Note: The norm of any normalized vector is 1. Specifically, the norm of any normalized eigenvector is also 1.
