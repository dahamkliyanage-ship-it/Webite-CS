Linear Equations

Definition(Linear Equation)

A first order differential equation of the standard form,
$$ \frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x), $$
is said to be a linear equation in the dependent variable $y$.

  • If $Q(x) = 0$, then the equation is called a homogeneous linear differential equation.
  • If $Q(x) \neq 0$, then the equation is called a nonhomogeneous linear differential equation.
Method of Solving using an Integrating Factor

  1. Put the linear equation into the standard form: $\frac{dy}{dx} + P(x)y = Q(x)$.
  2. Identify $P(x)$ and then find the integrating factor: $e^{\int P(x)dx}$.
  3. Multiply both sides of the standard form of the equation by the integrating factor. The left hand side of the resulting equation is automatically the derivative of the product of the integrating factor and $y$, i.e.,
    $$ \frac{d}{dx}\left[ y e^{\int P(x)dx} \right] = e^{\int P(x)dx}Q(x). $$
  4. Integrate both sides of the resulting equation with respect to $x$ to solve for $y$.
Worked Example: Integrating Factor

Solve the linear equation $\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{2}{x}y = 3$.

Step 1: Standard form.
The equation is already in standard form with $P(x) = \frac{2}{x}$ and $Q(x) = 3$.

Step 2: Integrating factor (IF).
$$ \text{IF} = e^{\int \frac{2}{x} dx} = e^{2\ln|x|} = e^{\ln(x^2)} = x^2 $$

Step 3: Multiply by IF.
$$ x^2\left(\frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{2}{x}y\right) = 3x^2 $$ $$ x^2\frac{dy}{dx} + 2xy = 3x^2 $$
The left side is exactly the derivative of $(y \cdot x^2)$:
$$ \frac{d}{dx}(y \cdot x^2) = 3x^2 $$

Step 4: Integrate both sides.
$$ \int \frac{d}{dx}(y \cdot x^2) dx = \int 3x^2 dx $$ $$ y \cdot x^2 = x^3 + C $$ $$ y = x + \frac{C}{x^2} $$
