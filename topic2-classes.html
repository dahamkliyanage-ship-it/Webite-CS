Topic 02

The Main Classes of Materials

At present, there are over 60,000 materials available for engineers to use, which are generally divided into three main classes: Metals, Polymers, and Ceramics.

1. Metals & Alloys:

  • Examples: Pure metals like Iron, Copper (electrical wires), and Aluminum (beverage cans); and alloys like Steel (Fe+C), Brass (Cu+Zn), and Duralumin (Al+Cu).
  • Properties: Metals generally feature good electrical and thermal conductivity, high strength, high stiffness, and good ductility. Some are magnetic, and some can even become superconductors at extremely low temperatures.

2. Polymers (Plastics & Rubber):

  • Examples: Plastics like PVC, Polyethylene (PE), and Teflon (PTFE); and rubbers like Natural Rubber and Styrene Butadiene Rubber (SBR).
  • Properties: Polymers are macromolecules usually based on a carbon backbone. They are lightweight, corrosion-resistant, inexpensive, and easy to process at low temperatures. While they have low strength, they exhibit high toughness and act as excellent electrical and thermal insulators.

3. Ceramics & Glasses:

  • Examples: Traditional ceramics like pottery, bricks, and porcelain; and advanced ceramics like Silicon carbide, Alumina, and Zirconia.
  • Properties: Ceramics are inorganic, non-metallic materials manufactured by shaping and then firing. They boast high melting temperatures, low density, high strength, high stiffness, high hardness, and excellent corrosion resistance.
  • Drawback: The one major drawback of ceramics (which are crystalline) and glasses (which are amorphous) is that they are extremely brittle.
