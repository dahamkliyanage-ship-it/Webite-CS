1. 2D Particle Kinematics (Plane-Polar Coordinates)

Kinematics describes purely the geometric aspects of motion without reference to forces.

Velocity Components:
Radial Velocity: vr = dr/dt = ṙ
Transverse Velocity: vθ = r · (dθ/dt) = r · θ̇  [vθ = θ̇ × r]
Total Velocity: v = √(vr² + vθ²)

Acceleration Components:
Radial Accel: ar = r̈ - r · θ̇²  [Centripetal: θ̇ × (θ̇ × r) acts towards centre, hence minus sign]
Transverse Accel: aθ = r · θ̈ + 2 · ṙ · θ̇  [Coriolis component: 2(θ̇ × ṙ)]
Total Accel: a = √(ar² + aθ²)
  • Radial Velocity (ṙ): Rate at which the position vector lengthens or shortens along the radial line.
  • Transverse Velocity (rθ̇): Tangential velocity perpendicular to the radial arm due to angular motion.
  • Centripetal Component (-rθ̇²): Directed inwards toward the origin O.
  • Coriolis Component (2ṙθ̇): Arises whenever a slider moves with speed ṙ along an arm that is concurrently rotating with angular speed θ̇.
Quick Test: Particle Polar Kinematics

A slider moves outward along a rotating radial arm at a constant sliding speed of 2 m/s. If the arm rotates at a constant angular velocity of 5 rad/s, what is the magnitude of the Coriolis acceleration?

Show Answer & Working

Coriolis acceleration acoriolis = 2 · θ̇ · ṙ = 2 × 5 × 2 = 20 m/s².

2. Categories of Rigid-Body Plane Motion

  • Rectilinear Translation: All points trace straight parallel lines; orientation remains fixed (ω = 0).
  • Curvilinear Translation: Points move along congruent curved trajectories without rotating (ω = 0).
  • Fixed-Axis Rotation: Body rotates about an immovable axis; velocity varies linearly with radius (v = rω).
  • General Plane Motion: Simultaneous combination of translation and rotation (e.g., connecting rod in an engine).
Relative Velocity: vB = vA + vB/A = vA + (ω × rB/A)
Relative Acceleration: aB = aA + (α × rB/A) - ω² · rB/A

3. Instantaneous Centre of Rotation (ICR)

At any given instant, general planar motion can be modeled as pure rotation about a single point of zero instantaneous velocity (point I).

vA = (AI) · ω
vB = (BI) · ω
ω = vA / (AI) = vB / (BI)
  • Location Method: The ICR lies at the intersection of perpendicular lines drawn to the velocity vectors at two distinct points.
  • Centrode: The complete locus traced by instantaneous centres over time.
  • Pure Rolling Contact: For a wheel rolling without slipping, the contact point on the track is the ICR (vcontact = 0, vcentre = rω, vtop = 2rω).
Quick Test: ICR Rolling Wheel

A cylinder of radius 0.2 m rolls without slipping on a fixed horizontal ground. If the centre moves forward at 2 m/s, what is the velocity of the point at the topmost perimeter?

Show Answer & Working

1. Ground contact is the ICR: vcentre = r · ω → ω = 2 / 0.2 = 10 rad/s.

2. Topmost point distance from ICR is 2r: vtop = (2r) · ω = 2 × 2 = 4 m/s.

4. Velocity Vector Diagrams

Constructed progressively across links by plotting relative velocity vectors perpendicular to rigid link segments.

  • Notation: Vector ab represents the velocity of point B relative to point A (vB/A).
  • Magnitude: |ab| = (AB) · ωAB, directed strictly perpendicular to link AB.
  • Kinematic Similarity (Rigid Body Triangle): For any points A, B, and C on the same rigid link, the velocity diagram forms a geometrically similar triangle:
    ab / AB = bc / BC = ac / AC = ω
  • Intermediate Points / Centre of Mass (G): If point G lies along the physical link AB, it divides the velocity vector in the exact same proportion:
    ag / ab = AG / AB  →  Velocity of G: vG = og

5. Acceleration Vector Diagrams

Relative acceleration between two points A and B on a rigid link consists of two orthogonal components:

Radial (Centripetal): a1x1 = (AB) · ω²  [Parallel to AB, directed towards centre A]
Transverse (Tangential): x1b1 = (AB) · α  [Perpendicular to link AB]
Total Relative Accel: a1b1 = a1x1 + x1b1
Magnitude: |a1b1| = (AB) · √(α² + ω⁴)
Phase Angle: φ = tan⁻¹(α / ω²)
  • Intermediate Points / Centre of Mass (G): Just like velocities, intermediate point accelerations divide the relative vector proportionally:
    a1g1 / a1b1 = AG / AB  →  Absolute Accel of G: aG = o1g1

6. Mobility (Grubler's Criterion) & Epicyclic Gear Trains

Degrees of Freedom (DOF) determines the independent inputs needed to control a mechanism.

Grubler's Criterion: F = 3(N - 1) - 2L - H
Condition for Mechanism: F = 1  →  3N - 2L - H = 4
Grashof's Inversion Condition: s + l ≤ p + q
  • N, L, H: Total links including fixed frame (N), lower pairs (L: 1 DOF pins/sliders), higher pairs (H: 2 DOFs gear meshing/cam contact).
  • Mobility States:
    • F = 0: Statically determinate rigid structure (no motion possible).
    • F = 1: Constrained kinematic mechanism with a single defined output per input.
    • F > 1: Unconstrained multi-degree system requiring multiple coordinated inputs.
  • Grashof Theorem: If shortest (s) + longest (l) ≤ other two links (p + q), at least one link can make continuous 360° revolutions.
External Meshing: ωP = (1 + rS/rP) · ωL - (rS/rP) · ωS
Internal Meshing (Annulus): ωP = (1 - rA/rP) · ωL + (rA/rP) · ωA
  • S, P, L, A: Sun wheel (S), Planet wheel (P), Arm/Carrier (L), Annulus outer ring (A).
  • Relative speed transmission allows compact, high-torque reduction gearboxes.

Quick Test: Grubler's Mobility

A planar four-bar mechanism has 4 links and 4 revolute pin joints. Using Grubler's criterion, calculate its degrees of freedom (F).

Show Answer & Working

N = 4, L = 4, H = 0.

F = 3(N - 1) - 2L - H = 3(4 - 1) - 2(4) - 0 = 9 - 8 = 1. Therefore, F = 1 (single-DOF mechanism).
