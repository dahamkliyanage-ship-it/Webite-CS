Topic 02

Energy Band Structure

In isolated atoms, electrons occupy well-defined, discrete energy states. However, when atoms are brought closely together to form a solid, their atomic orbitals overlap. This causes the discrete energy levels to split into closely spaced levels, forming continuous Energy Bands[cite: 11].

  • Valence Band: The highest energy band that is occupied by electrons at absolute zero (0 K)[cite: 11].
  • Conduction Band: The empty band just above the valence band. Electrons must jump to this band to move freely and conduct electricity[cite: 11].

The Fermi Level

At 0 Kelvin, electrons settle into the lowest available energy states, building a "Fermi Sea"[cite: 11]. The Fermi Energy is the exact surface of this sea[cite: 11]. The Fermi Level is formally defined as the energy level that has a 50% probability of electron occupancy (based on the Fermi-Dirac distribution)[cite: 11].

Metals vs. Insulators

  • Metals: The valence band and conduction band overlap (or the Fermi level sits inside a partially filled band). Minimal energy is needed to promote an electron into the conduction band[cite: 11].
  • Insulators & Semiconductors: A massive "band gap" exists between the valence and conduction bands. In insulators, the gap is too large to cross. In semiconductors, the gap is small enough that higher temperatures can provide enough thermal energy for electrons to jump across[cite: 11].
