Matrices

Introduction

Definition:

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers arranged in rows and columns and enclosed by a pair of brackets.
Example 1.
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ -1 & 5 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 & 6 \\ 7 & 9 & 3 \\ 2 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} $$

If the matrix has $m$ rows and $n$ columns it is called an $m \times n$ matrix or a matrix of order $m \times n$. A matrix is denoted by a single capital letter. If matrix $A$ has $m$ rows and $n$ columns it is denoted by $A_{m \times n}$. Consider the following matrix:

$$ A = \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \cdot & \cdot & a_{1n} \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ a_{i1} & a_{i2} & \cdot & a_{ij} & a_{in} \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & \cdot & a_{mj} & a_{mn} \end{pmatrix} $$

It can be represented as $A = (a_{ij})_{m \times n}$.

5.2 Some Special Matrices

5.2.1 Square Matrices

A matrix is called a square matrix when the number of rows equals the number of columns ($m = n$).
$$ \begin{pmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \cdot & \cdot & a_{1n} \\ a_{21} & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & a_{2n} \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot \\ a_{n1} & a_{n2} & \cdot & \cdot & a_{nn} \end{pmatrix} $$

In a square matrix, the elements having equal subscripts, $a_{11}, a_{22}, \cdots, a_{nn}$, form the main diagonal of the matrix.

Zero Matrix

A matrix whose elements are all zero is called a zero matrix or null matrix and it is denoted by $O_{m \times n}$ where $m$ is the number of rows and $n$ is the number of columns. That is $a_{ij} = 0$ for all $i$ and $j$.
Example 2.
$$ O_{3 \times 3} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} $$

Diagonal matrix

A square matrix whose only non-zero elements are diagonal elements is called a diagonal matrix.
Example 3.
$$ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix} $$

5.2.4 Identity matrix (Unit matrix)

A diagonal matrix whose diagonal elements are all equal to 1 is called an identity matrix or a unit matrix and is denoted by $I$.
Example 4.
$$ I_{3 \times 3} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} $$

Column matrix (Column vector) and Row matrix (Row vector)

A column matrix is a matrix having only one column while a row matrix is a matrix having only one row.
Example 5. $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$ is a row matrix and $B = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is a column matrix.

5.2.6 Triangular matrix

  • A square matrix $A = (a_{ij})_{n \times n}$ is called an upper triangular matrix if all its elements below the diagonal are zero.
  • A square matrix $A = (a_{ij})_{n \times n}$ is called a lower triangular matrix if all its elements above the diagonal are zero.
  • A matrix is called a triangular matrix if it is upper or lower triangular.
Example 6. $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & -3 \\ 0 & -2 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$ is an upper triangular matrix and $B = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is a lower triangular matrix.

5.2.7 Transpose of a matrix

The matrix obtained from a given matrix by interchanging its rows and columns is called the transpose of the matrix. For a given matrix $A$, the transpose of $A$ is denoted by $A^T$.
Example 7. If $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ then $A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$.
