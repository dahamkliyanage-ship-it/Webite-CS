Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of Special Matrices

Theorem: Suppose $A$ is an $n \times n$ real symmetric matrix. Then the following hold:

  • (i). The eigenvalues of $A$ are all real numbers.
  • (ii). The eigenvectors of $A$ corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are mutually orthogonal.

Proof. Omit.

Detail & Example: Real symmetric matrices ($A = A^T$) guarantee that we won't end up with complex eigenvalues. Furthermore, if you calculate the eigenvectors for different eigenvalues, their dot product will always be exactly zero.
Theorem: Suppose $A$ is an upper triangular matrix. Then the eigenvalues of $A$ are the diagonal entries of $A$.

Proof. Omit.

Worked Example: Consider the upper triangular matrix $A$:
$$ A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 7 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & -3 \end{pmatrix} $$
Because all entries below the main diagonal are zero, you do not need to solve the characteristic polynomial $(4-\lambda)(1-\lambda)(-3-\lambda) = 0$. You can immediately state the eigenvalues by looking at the main diagonal:
$$ \lambda_1 = 4, \quad \lambda_2 = 1, \quad \lambda_3 = -3 $$
Definition: Suppose $A$ is a square matrix. The trace of $A$ is the sum of its diagonal entries, which is denoted by $\text{trace}(A)$ or $\text{Tr}(A)$. That is
$$ \text{trace}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ii} \quad \text{where } A = (a_{ij}). $$
Theorem: If $\lambda_1, \cdots, \lambda_n$ are the eigenvalues of $A = (a_{ij})$ then
$$ \text{trace}(A) = \lambda_1 + \cdots + \lambda_n. $$
Proof. Omit.

Remark. In above theorem, the eigenvalues can be either real or complex.

Worked Example: Verifying the Trace Theorem
Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

Step 1: Compute the trace of $A$.
Summing the main diagonal entries: $\text{trace}(A) = 2 + 2 = 4$.

Step 2: Find the eigenvalues.
The characteristic equation is $\det(A - \lambda I) = (2-\lambda)(2-\lambda) - (-1)(-1) = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 = 0$.
Factoring this gives $(\lambda - 3)(\lambda - 1) = 0$. The eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 3$ and $\lambda_2 = 1$.

Step 3: Sum the eigenvalues.
Sum of eigenvalues $= 3 + 1 = 4$.
This perfectly matches the trace of the matrix, confirming Theorem 10.3.3. This is an extremely useful shortcut to verify if your calculated eigenvalues are correct!
