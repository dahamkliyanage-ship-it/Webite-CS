Wronskian

Consider the differential equation $y'' + Py' + Qy = 0$ where $P$ and $Q$ are functions of $x$ alone.

Definition(Linear Independence)

  • Two solutions $u(x), v(x)$ of a differential equation are said to be linearly dependent, if there exist constants $c_1$ and $c_2$, not both zero, such that $c_1u(x) + c_2v(x) = 0$.
  • Otherwise, i.e., $c_1 = c_2 = 0$, the solution is independent.

Definition(The Wronskian)

The Wronskian of two solutions $u(x), v(x)$ of a differential equation, is defined to be the determinant:
$$ w(x) = \begin{vmatrix} u(x) & v(x) \\ u'(x) & v'(x) \end{vmatrix} = u(x)v'(x) - v(x)u'(x) $$
Theorem:

The Wronskian of two solutions of the differential equation (28) is identically zero or never zero.

Again let $u$ and $v$ be solutions of the second order linear differential equation $y'' + P(x)y' + Q(x)y = 0$. Then:
$$ W(u, v) = c e^{-\int P(x)dx} = W(u, v)(x_0)e^{-\int P(x)dx} $$

Theorem:

The solutions of differential equation (28) are linearly dependent if and only if their Wronskian vanish identically.

Variation of Parameters

By our work above, to find the general solution of the nonhomogeneous equation we needed to find a linearly independent pair of solutions $y_1, y_2$ of the homogeneous equation, and a particular solution $y_p$. The method of variation of parameters uses a pair of linearly independent solutions of the homogeneous equation to construct a particular solution.

Deriving the System

  1. We replace the arbitrary constants $C_1$ and $C_2$ by functions $u = u(x)$ and $v = v(x)$, which are to be determined so that $y_p(x) = u(x)y_1(x) + v(x)y_2(x)$ is a particular solution of the nonhomogeneous equation.
  2. Since there are two unknowns $u$ and $v$ to be determined we shall impose two conditions on these unknowns.
  3. For our second condition on $u$ and $v$, we set $y_1u' + y_2v' = 0$. This condition is chosen because it simplifies the first derivative $y_p'$ and because it will lead to a simple pair of equations in the unknowns $u$ and $v$.
  4. Now substitute $y_p, y_p'$ and $y_p''$ into the left side of equation (27). The condition that $y_p$ should satisfy is $y_1'u' + y_2'v' = f(x)$.

These constitute a system of two equations in the two unknowns $u'$ and $v'$:
$$ y_1u' + y_2v' = 0 $$ $$ y_1'u' + y_2'v' = f(x) $$
Integrating for the Final Solution

Solving for $u'$ and $v'$, we find that:
$$ u' = \frac{-y_2 f}{y_1y_2' - y_2y_1'} \quad \text{and} \quad v' = \frac{y_1 f}{y_1y_2' - y_2y_1'} $$
We know that the denominators here are non-zero because the expression $y_1(x)y_2'(x) - y_2(x)y_1'(x) = W(x)$ is the Wronskian of $y_1$ and $y_2$, and $y_1, y_2$ are linearly independent solutions of the homogeneous equation.

We can now get $u$ and $v$ by integrating:
$$ u = -\int \frac{y_2(x)f(x)}{W(x)}dx \quad \text{and} \quad v = \int \frac{y_1(x)f(x)}{W(x)}dx $$
Finally, a particular solution of the nonhomogeneous equation is:
$$ y_p(x) = -y_1(x)\int \frac{y_2(x)f(x)}{W(x)}dx + y_2(x)\int \frac{y_1(x)f(x)}{W(x)}dx $$
Worked Example: Variation of Parameters

Find the general solution to the differential equation $y'' + y = \sec(x)$.

Step 1: Solve the homogeneous equation to find $y_1$ and $y_2$.
The auxiliary equation for $y'' + y = 0$ is $m^2 + 1 = 0 \implies m = \pm i$.
The complementary solution is $y_c = C_1\cos(x) + C_2\sin(x)$.
Therefore, $y_1 = \cos(x)$ and $y_2 = \sin(x)$.

Step 2: Calculate the Wronskian $W(x)$.
$$ W(x) = \begin{vmatrix} \cos(x) & \sin(x) \\ -\sin(x) & \cos(x) \end{vmatrix} = (\cos(x))(\cos(x)) - (\sin(x))(-\sin(x)) = \cos^2(x) + \sin^2(x) = 1 $$

Step 3: Apply the integration formulas for $u$ and $v$.
Here, $f(x) = \sec(x)$.
$$ u = -\int \frac{\sin(x)\sec(x)}{1}dx = -\int \tan(x)dx = \ln|\cos(x)| $$ $$ v = \int \frac{\cos(x)\sec(x)}{1}dx = \int 1 dx = x $$

Step 4: Construct the particular and general solutions.
The particular solution is $y_p = u y_1 + v y_2 = (\ln|\cos(x)|)\cos(x) + x\sin(x)$.
The general solution is $y = y_c + y_p$:
$$ y = C_1\cos(x) + C_2\sin(x) + \cos(x)\ln|\cos(x)| + x\sin(x) $$
