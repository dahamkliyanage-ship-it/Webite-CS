The Shortest Distance Between an Arbitrary Point and a Plane

Problem Setup

Suppose $(x, y, z)$ is a point on the plane $ax + by + cz = d$. Let $(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ be an arbitrary point in the 3-space located outside the plane.

Our objective is to find the minimum (perpendicular) distance from this external point down to the plane.

Geometric Insight: The shortest distance from any point to a plane is measured along a line segment that is orthogonal (perpendicular) to the plane. By utilizing the plane's normal vector, we can project the vector connecting a point on the plane to our external point directly onto that normal vector.

Derivation & Distance Formula

Theorem:

Clearly, $\underline{n} = a\underline{i} + b\underline{j} + c\underline{k}$ is perpendicular to the plane. Therefore, the shortest distance is obtained by taking the absolute scalar projection of the vector $(\underline{r_1} - \underline{r_0})$ onto the normal vector $\underline{n}$.

Thus,
$$ \text{the distance} = \left| (\underline{r_1} - \underline{r_0}) \cdot \frac{\underline{n}}{|\underline{n}|} \right| = \frac{|(\underline{r_1} - \underline{r_0}) \cdot \underline{n}|}{|\underline{n}|} $$

Alternative Cartesian Formula: If you expand this dot product using point coordinates $(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and plane equation $ax + by + cz = d$, it simplifies directly to the classic coordinate geometry formula:
$$ D = \frac{|ax_1 + by_1 + cz_1 - d|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}} $$

Detailed Mathematical Example

Example: Calculating Point-to-Plane Distance

Find the shortest distance from the point $P(1, -2, 3)$ to the plane given by $2x + 3y - 6z = 5$.

Step 1: Identify the plane coefficients and point coordinates.
  • Plane parameters: $a = 2$, $b = 3$, $c = -6$, $d = 5$
  • External point: $x_1 = 1$, $y_1 = -2$, $z_1 = 3$
Step 2: Substitute the point into the left side of the plane equation (numerator).
$$ ax_1 + by_1 + cz_1 - d = 2(1) + 3(-2) + (-6)(3) - 5 $$ $$ = 2 - 6 - 18 - 5 = -27 $$
Step 3: Calculate the magnitude of the normal vector (denominator).
$$ |\underline{n}| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2} = \sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7 $$
Step 4: Compute the final distance.
$$ D = \frac{|-27|}{7} = \frac{27}{7} \approx 3.86 \text{ units} $$
The shortest distance from the point $(1, -2, 3)$ to the plane is exactly $\frac{27}{7}$ units.
