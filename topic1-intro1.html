Topic 01

Intro to Nanoscale & Nature

Nanoscience is the study of structures and materials on the scale of nanometers, while Nanotechnology is the development of materials and devices by exploiting the characteristics of particles on this scale.

What is a Nanomaterial?

According to the ISO definition, a nanomaterial is a material with any external dimension in the nanoscale (approximately 1 to 100 nm, where 1 nm = 10-9 m), or one having an internal/surface structure in the nanoscale.

  • Nano Objects: Single units like a Bucky ball (C60) or a Carbon nanotube.
  • Nanostructured Materials: Larger materials with nano-level internal structures, like TiO2 nanotube thin films.

Why Nano?

At the nanoscale, materials exhibit highly unusual and interesting properties compared to their bulk counterparts. For example, bulk graphite has a Young's modulus of ~15 GPa, but a single nanoscale sheet of Graphene has a Young's modulus of ~1000 GPa! Carrier mobility also skyrockets from ~30-50 cm²/V·s in bulk metals to ~104 cm²/V·s in single-layer graphene.

Nanotechnology in Nature

Nature has been utilizing nanotechnology for millions of years:

  • The Lotus Effect: The leaves of the lotus plant are superhydrophobic due to nanoscale bumps, achieving a water contact angle greater than 90° (often ~146°), which prevents wetting.
  • Gecko Adhesive System: Geckos can walk on walls because their feet possess 1 million micro-hairs (setae), which branch off into 1000 nanoscale spatular tips, generating massive adhesion forces.
